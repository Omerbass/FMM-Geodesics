\noindent
These notes develop the $D=3$ planar antiferromagnetic spherical model as a replacement for
the mean-field antiferromagnetic Ising example in the manuscript. Reference [K] is Knops,
Phys.\ Rev.\ B \textbf{8}, 4209 (1973); equation numbers of the form (K2.39) refer to that
paper. Reference [BR] is the current draft (Basri--Raz).

$D$ denotes the spatial dimension; $\LK$ denotes Knops' sublattice parameter (his $\lambda$),
reserving $\lambda$ for the control vector. Constraints are imposed \emph{strictly},
configuration by configuration; \S\ref{sec:expfamily} explains why.

\section{The Plan:}

\subsubsection{Proposed structure}

\textbf{§0. Notation and conventions.} Short but necessary: Knops' sign convention is inverted (positive J = antiferromagnetic), and you have a three-way collision on $\beta$ (inverse temperature, order-parameter exponent, and it appears inside $b$). Fix $\lambda = (\beta, \beta H, \beta H'_{\parallel}, \beta H_{\perp})$ as natural variables conjugate to (-E, M, $M'_{\parallel},M'_{\perp}$) up front, since the metric definition assumes the Hamiltonian is linear in $\lambda$.

\textbf{§1. The model.} Bipartite cubic lattice, mean spherical model with two spherical fields $z_A$, $z_B$ (Knops §II), then the planar generalization (§IV): two-component spins, transverse staggered field $H'_\perp$, order parameter $x^2$ identified with the transverse staggered magnetization via (4.8). Motivation subsection should say explicitly why planar rather than scalar: the scalar SM has no spontaneous staggered magnetization at $H \neq 0$, so its "order parameter" $x$ has no thermodynamic conjugate and no field to drive it. The planar version restores that, mimics the n-vector antiferromagnet, and gives you the spin-flop physics --- while keeping the phase boundary and all longitudinal averages identical to the exactly-solved scalar case.

\textbf{§2. Massieu potential.} $\psi = \ln Z$ in the natural variables, both regions. Key content: the constraint equations (2.27)–(2.28) / (4.3)–(4.4), the elimination giving $\lambda  = (H + H')/(H - H'), M'(H, H')$ from (2.43), the phase boundary (2.39)--(2.40) and its H' = 0 reduction, and the Watson integral $W_3$. Then all second derivatives of $\psi$, distinguishing the \textit{constrained} (spherical) susceptibilities from the MSM ones — Knops' (2.36)--(2.38) point is essential here and is a real trap: \\$\chi_{MSM}$ is infinite throughout the ordered region while the physical $\chi '$ is finite.

\textbf{§3. Model A dynamics.} Justify non-conserved dynamics (a protocol that varies H must change M, so uniform magnetization cannot be conserved; also matches the Glauber choice in the current manuscript). Langevin equations for S\_k plus the \textit{dynamics of the spherical fields}. This is the technical heart and where real work is needed: the constraint couples every mode, so the relaxation of the macroscopic variables is not a simple exponential and $T_{\mu\nu}$ is not a naive $\tau \times$ Hessian. Expect a memory kernel / branch cut and possibly power-law tails. Two things must be established here: that $z = 2$ for the order-parameter modes, and that $\int_0^{\infty} C(t) dt$ converges — non-trivial because the ordered phase of the spherical model is gapless throughout ($z_A$  $z_B$ sticks at $\beta^2\hat{J}^2(0))$, so slow modes exist everywhere below the boundary, not just on it.

\textbf{§4. The metric.} $g_{\mu\nu}$ as a Brillouin-zone integral of (per-mode susceptibility) × (per-mode relaxation time), with the constraint contributions. Explicit forms in the disordered region and the ordered region; check rank and positive-definiteness; compare with the mean-field $2\times 2$ metric of the current §e to show what the degeneracy was an artifact of.

\textbf{§5. Behavior across the transition.} Sub-parts: (a) generic boundary point, $H \neq 0$ --- singular part of the Hessian $\sim \varepsilon^{-\alpha} $ together with $\tau  \sim \varepsilon^{-z\nu}$, giving $g \sim ε^{-1}$, $\sqrt{g} \sim \varepsilon^{-1/2}$, finite length; (b) the $H = 0$ line, where the staggered susceptibility diverges and the answer differs; (c) reconciliation with the Widom-scaling criterion of the manuscript. Point (c) is worth its own emphasis: when $\alpha < 0$ the analytic background dominates the Hessian, so "$\tau\times$ full Hessian" and "$\tau\times$ singular part" give different answers (log-divergent vs. convergent). The exact solution settles which decomposition is correct, and that validates --- or corrects --- the general criterion in your §c.

\textbf{§6. Geometry of the control space.} Which 2D slice to use. At H' = 0 the ordered region is a dome attached to the T axis, so it cannot be circumvented — the direct analogue of your Fig. 1. But at fixed T the ordered region is a \textit{closed} curve in the (H, H') plane (Knops Fig. 1), so it can be encircled, and "around vs. through" becomes a sharp, quantitative question that the mean-field example could not pose. I'd draft both.

\textbf{§7. Minimal paths.} Fast marching on the numerically evaluated metric, as in Rotskoff–Crooks; re-derive the classification of optimal paths and check whether the "cross and come back" phenomenon survives without the null directions.

\textbf{§8. What this changes in the manuscript.} A short mapping from these results onto the sections of the current draft, including a new row for the 3D spherical model in Table I.

\textbf{Appendices.} Watson integral and $T_0$; explicit derivatives of $\psi$; the linearized dynamical matrix; details of the constraint-mode calculation.

\section{Conventions}
%===============================================================

\subsection{Lattice}
Simple cubic in $D=3$, $N=L^3$ sites, unit lattice constant, periodic boundary conditions.
Bipartite: $\brr_j\in A$ if $\sum_\mu(r_j)_\mu$ is even, $\in B$ if odd; each sublattice has
$N/2$ sites. Write
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_j\equiv e^{i\bQ\cdot\brr_j}=\pm1,\qquad \bQ\equiv(\pi,\pi,\pi).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Rescaled couplings}
\label{sec:rescale}
Knops writes the planar energy (K4.1) with explicit factors of $2$ on the coupling and the
field. The reason is that the planar Gaussian trace runs over \emph{two} components while
the spherical radius per site is still one; Knops rescales the exchange and the uniform
field precisely so that the longitudinal saddle equations and the phase boundary coincide
with those of the scalar model. In the zero-field symmetric state this amounts to each
component carrying half of the mean-square spin length.

%\FLAG{Do not over-read this.} In a nonzero $z$-directed field the components are \emph{not} equipartitioned: from the Gaussian averages together with the constraint (\S\ref{sec:saddle}, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:M}--\eqref{eq:constraint}), $\avg{(\sigma^x)^2}=\tfrac12\Wc+x^2$ and $\avg{(\sigma^z)^2}=\tfrac12\Wc+M^2$, equal only when $M^2=x^2$. Only the sum is pinned, $\avg{|\bm\sigma_j|^2}=1$. The factors of two are correct regardless; an earlier draft justified them by asserting $\avg{(\sigma^z)^2}=\tfrac12$ generally, which is false.

Carrying the $2$'s around is a reliable source of error --- in particular the control
parameter conjugate to the magnetisation is $2\beta H$, not $\beta H$ --- so absorb them:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \Jc\equiv 2J,\qquad \Hc\equiv 2H\ }
\end{equation}
The energy is then, with a \emph{bond} sum (each unordered nearest-neighbour pair once),
\begin{equation}
  E=\underbrace{\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\Jc_{ij}\,\bm\sigma_i\cdot\bm\sigma_j}_{\textstyle E_{\rm int}}
    \;-\;\Hc\sum_j\sigma^z_j,
  \qquad
  \Jc_{ij}=\Jc>0\ \text{ for $i\in A$, $j\in B$ n.n.}
  \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
Following [K] the sign convention is the opposite of the usual one: \emph{positive} $\Jc$
is antiferromagnetic. Flag this in the manuscript, which uses $K=-J$ with $J<0$.

\subsection{Fourier transform}
Symmetric convention, \textbf{full} first Brillouin zone:
\begin{equation}
  \bm\sigma_j=\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\sum_{\bk}e^{i\bk\cdot\brr_j}\bm S_{\bk},
  \qquad
  \bm S_{\bk}=\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\sum_j e^{-i\bk\cdot\brr_j}\bm\sigma_j,
  \label{eq:ft}
\end{equation}
$\bk=(2\pi/L)(l_x,l_y,l_z)$, exactly $N$ wavevectors. Then
\begin{equation}
  \sum_j e^{i(\bk-\bk')\cdot\brr_j}=N\delta_{\bk\bk'},
  \qquad
  \bm S_{-\bk}=\bm S^{*}_{\bk},
  \qquad
  \sum_j|\bm\sigma_j|^2=\sum_{\bk}|\bm S_{\bk}|^2,
  \label{eq:parseval}
\end{equation}
with $|\bm S_{\bk}|^2\equiv|S^x_{\bk}|^2+|S^z_{\bk}|^2$. Full zone, not the half zone of [K].

\subsection{The structure function}
\begin{equation}
  \Jc(\bk)\equiv\tfrac12\sum_{\bdel}e^{-i\bk\cdot\bdel}\Jc_{\bdel}
   =\Jc\big(\cos k_x+\cos k_y+\cos k_z\big),
  \qquad \Jc(0)=3\Jc\equiv P .
  \label{eq:structure}
\end{equation}
The $\tfrac12$ is fixed by requiring the bond sum to diagonalise with unit coefficient:
writing the bond sum as half the unrestricted sum and inserting \eqref{eq:ft},
\begin{align}
  \sum_j\bm\sigma_j\cdot\bm\sigma_{j+\bdel}
  &=\frac1N\sum_j\sum_{\bk,\bk'}e^{i\bk\cdot\brr_j}e^{i\bk'\cdot(\brr_j+\bdel)}\bm S_{\bk}\cdot\bm S_{\bk'}
   =\sum_{\bk}e^{-i\bk\cdot\bdel}|\bm S_{\bk}|^2,
\end{align}
so that
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ E_{\rm int}=\sum_{\bk}\Jc(\bk)\,|\bm S_{\bk}|^2\ }
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \sum_j\sigma^z_j=\sqrt N\,S^z_0 .
  \label{eq:diag}
\end{equation}
%Reading Knops' $\sum_{\vec i,\vec j}$ as an unrestricted double sum would give $2\Jc(\bk)$ and $\kB T_0=4/W_3$ instead of $2/W_3$; the bond reading reproduces (K2.29).

\subsection{Bipartite identity}
Since $\cos(\pi-k_\mu)=-\cos k_\mu$,
\begin{equation}
  \Jc(\bQ-\bk)=-\Jc(\bk),
  \label{eq:bipartite}
\end{equation}
which is (K2.11); $\Jc(\bQ)=-P =-3\mathcal{J}$ is the minimum, so the ordering wavevector is $\bQ$. Note
$2\bQ$ is a reciprocal lattice vector, so $\bQ\equiv-\bQ$ and $\bm S_{\bQ}$ is real. Near
the corner, with $\bk=\bQ-\bq$ (assuming that $\vec q$ is a small number and expanding in it),
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \Jc(\bQ-\bq)=-P+\tfrac{\Jc}{2}\,q^2+O(q^4)\ }
  \label{eq:smallq}
\end{equation}
The consequences for the mode stiffness $a_{\bk}$ are drawn in \S\ref{sec:nu}, once $z$ has
been introduced.

\subsection{Control parameters}
$\beta\equiv1/\kB T$ always; the order-parameter exponent is $\beta_{\rm op}$. The Boltzmann
weight $e^{-\beta E}$ is
\begin{equation}
  \exp\Big[-\beta E_{\rm int}+h\sum_j\sigma^z_j\Big],
  \qquad h\equiv\beta\Hc=2\beta H,
\end{equation}
manifestly linear in
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \lambda=(\lambda^1,\lambda^2)=(\beta,h)\ }
  \qquad
  X_1=-E_{\rm int},\quad X_2=M_{\rm tot}=\sum_j\sigma^z_j ,
  \label{eq:controls}
\end{equation}
as required by [BR, Eq.~(1)]. Throughout $\psi\equiv\ln Z$. Both controls are
\emph{sublattice-symmetric}; this is used in \S\ref{sec:zminus}.

\section{The model}
%===============================================================

\subsection{Spins and constraints}
Each site carries a planar real spin $\bm\sigma_j=(\sigma^x_j,\sigma^z_j)\in\mathbb R^2$.
The spherical constraints are imposed separately on each sublattice and configuration by
configuration:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\
  Q_A\equiv\sum_{j\in A}|\bm\sigma_j|^2=\tfrac N2,
  \qquad
  Q_B\equiv\sum_{j\in B}|\bm\sigma_j|^2=\tfrac N2 \ }
  \label{eq:constraints}
\end{equation}
This is (K4.2). Configuration space is $S^{nN/2-1}\times S^{nN/2-1}$ with $n=2$, and
\begin{equation}
  P_\lambda[\bm\sigma]=\frac{1}{Z(\lambda)}\,e^{\lambda^\mu X_\mu(\bm\sigma)}\,d\nu(\bm\sigma),
  \qquad
  d\nu=\delta\big(Q_A-\tfrac N2\big)\,\delta\big(Q_B-\tfrac N2\big)\prod_j d^2\bm\sigma_j .
  \label{eq:measure}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exponential-family structure: why strict}
\label{sec:expfamily}
The base measure $d\nu$ \textbf{does not depend on $\lambda$}. Hence \eqref{eq:measure} is a
genuine exponential family with natural parameters $\lambda$ and sufficient statistics
$X=(-E_{\rm int},M_{\rm tot})$, and
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\
  \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\lambda^\mu}=\avg{X_\mu},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial\lambda^\mu\partial\lambda^\nu}=\mathrm{Cov}\big(X_\mu,X_\nu\big) \ }
  \label{eq:expfam}
\end{equation}
hold \emph{exactly, at finite $N$}, with no approximation. The second derivative of the
Massieu potential \emph{is} the covariance in the physical constrained ensemble.

%\FLAG{Order of limits --- important.} \eqref{eq:expfam} is a finite-$N$ identity, but everything from \S\ref{sec:saddle} onward is a \emph{thermodynamic-limit} calculation. At finite $N$: $Z$ is analytic in $(\beta,h)$, there is no transition, the symmetric ensemble has $\avg{S^x_{\bQ}}=0$, and the saddle cannot sit at $a_{\bQ}=0$ because the Gaussian integral over that mode would diverge. The correct procedure is to remove the $\bQ$ mode from the determinant, let its occupation become macroscopic, and only then take $N\to\infty$:

%Removing one mode from an $N$-mode sum is $O(1/N)$ and therefore invisible in the density, which is why \eqref{eq:Phi} below may be written as an unrestricted integral --- but the \emph{order of limits} is what makes the condensate legitimate. Accordingly, everything called ``exact'' below \S\ref{sec:saddle} means exact for the thermodynamic-limit \emph{densities}, not at finite $N$.

\paragraph{Contrast with the mean spherical model.} There one imposes
$\avg{Q_A}=\avg{Q_B}=N/2$ via spherical fields $z_{A,B}(\lambda)$, so the base measure
carries $e^{-z_AQ_A-z_BQ_B}$ and \emph{does} depend on $\lambda$ (because in this case one has to find $z_A(\vec \lambda)$ and $z_B(\vec \lambda)$, they are not independent parameters); \eqref{eq:expfam} fails
and $\mathrm{Cov}_z\neq\partial^2\psi$. This is Knops' observation that $\chi'_{\rm MSM}$ is
infinite throughout the ordered region while the physical $\chi'$ is finite
(K2.36--2.38). In $D=3$ the discrepancy is not $O(1/N)$: $\mathrm{Var}(Q)/N\sim\int d^3q/q^4$
diverges in the ordered phase, so the two prescriptions differ at leading order, and
repairing it needs a conditioning formula which --- $Q$ being quadratic in the Gaussian
variables --- is only a large-$N$ regression. None of that arises here.

\paragraph{Consequence for \S3--\S4.} The dynamics must produce
$\int_0^\infty\avg{\delta X_\mu(t)\delta X_\nu(0)}dt$ in the constrained ensemble, whose
$t=0$ value is fixed by \eqref{eq:expfam}. That is a sharp target and a sharp check.

\subsection{Why planar and not scalar}
The scalar staggered magnetisation does have a formal conjugate field $H'$; the point is
subtler. In the scalar antiferromagnetic SM at $H\neq0$, the ordered phase is \emph{not}
represented by a nonzero spontaneous one-point staggered magnetisation ---
$\lim_{H'\to0}M'=0$ (below K2.43) --- even though long-range order survives in the
correlation functions, $g_{AA}(0)\propto x^2$ (K2.35).

The planar extension admits a conventional transverse staggered magnetisation. From
(K4.7--4.8), $\lim_{H'_\perp\to0}(S^\perp_A)^2=x^2/\LK$ and
$\lim_{H'_\perp\to0}(S^\perp_B)^2=x^2\LK$, so
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ x=\lim_{H'_\perp\to0^+}\ \lim_{N\to\infty}\ \frac{\avg{S^x_{\bQ}}}{\sqrt N}\ }
  \label{eq:xdef}
\end{equation}
is the order-parameter \emph{amplitude}. The order of limits is not decorative: at finite
$N$ the symmetric ensemble has $\avg{S^x_{\bQ}}=0$ (\S\ref{sec:expfamily}), so the
thermodynamic limit must be taken \emph{before} the field is removed. %Here $x$ ($x^2$ being the condensate density). 
The model then mimics the isotropic $n$-vector antiferromagnet,
spin-flop physics included, while keeping exact solubility and --- by the rescaling of
\S\ref{sec:rescale} --- the identical phase boundary and longitudinal averages of the scalar
case.

We subsequently restrict the control manifold to $H'_\perp=0$, so the ordering field
\emph{labels} the phase but is not itself driven. This is a deliberate choice with
consequences (\S\ref{sec:cost}); it is not a claim that the scalar model admits no
thermodynamic geometry.

\subsection{Why $H'=0$, and what it costs}
\label{sec:cost}
Setting the staggered field to zero buys a symmetric saddle (\S\ref{sec:zminus}), a
two-dimensional control manifold matching [BR, Fig.~1], and a closed-form critical line.

The cost is that no control parameter couples to the order parameter. In the finite-$N$,
symmetry-preserving ensemble the reflection $\bm\sigma\to(-\sigma^x,\sigma^z)$ is a symmetry
of \eqref{eq:energy}, so
\begin{equation}
  \avg{\delta M_{\rm tot}\,\delta S_\perp}=\avg{\delta E_{\rm int}\,\delta S_\perp}=0 ,
  \label{eq:decoupling}
\end{equation}
and this remains true throughout the disordered phase.

\FLAG{\eqref{eq:decoupling} fails in the selected ordered phase.} A pure state obtained via
$H'_\perp\to0^+$ does not respect the reflection, so odd correlators need not vanish.
Concretely, the exponential-family identity \eqref{eq:expfam} gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial x}{\partial\beta}
  =\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\,\mathrm{Cov}\big(S^x_{\bQ},\,-E_{\rm int}\big),
\end{equation}
and $x^2=1-t-M^2$ depends on $\beta$, so the covariance is manifestly nonzero. %An earlier draft used \eqref{eq:decoupling} to conclude that order-parameter fluctuations never enter; that conclusion is not available in the ordered phase.

The weaker statement that is actually needed, and is correct everywhere:
\begin{quote}
The control manifold $(\beta,h)$ contains no field conjugate to $S^x_{\bQ}$. Therefore the
order-parameter susceptibility $\chi_{\perp\perp}$ is not itself a component of the static
Hessian or of the metric. Critical order-parameter modes may nevertheless contribute
\emph{indirectly} to the autocorrelations of the energy-like observables.
\end{quote}

\FLAG{Where the nonzero covariance comes from.} The \emph{direct} coupling does vanish:
$\delta E_{\rm int}=\beta^{-1}\sum_{\bk}a_{\bk}\delta|\bm S_{\bk}|^2$ carries weight
$a_{\bQ}=0$ on the condensate mode. But $\delta S^x_{\bQ}$ is constraint-coupled to every
other mode through $Q_+=N$, and that induced correlation is what produces
$\partial x/\partial\beta\neq0$. \S4 must track this explicitly rather than appeal to
symmetry --- it is the same slaving that \S\ref{sec:qmode} identifies for the longitudinal
$\bQ$-mode.

\paragraph{Symmetry, correctly stated.} The term $-\Hc\sum_j\sigma^z_j$ breaks the planar
$O(2)$ explicitly down to the reflection $\sigma^x\to-\sigma^x$. Transverse staggered order
breaks that residual $Z_2$, so at $\Hc\neq0$ there is \textbf{no Goldstone mode}. Only at
$\Hc=0$ is $O(2)$ restored.

\paragraph{Gapless $\neq$ Goldstone.} It will emerge in \S\ref{sec:saddle} that the spherical
mass $r$ stays pinned at zero throughout the ordered phase, so the connected propagator is
massless and $a_{\bQ-\bq}\simeq cq^2$. These two statements are compatible and must be kept
apart: the masslessness is a special property of the spherical constraint (the multiplier
sticks at the edge of its range), \emph{not} a Goldstone mode, since at $\Hc\neq0$ the
uniform field has already reduced $O(2)$ to $Z_2$ and no continuous symmetry remains to be
spontaneously broken. This distinction matters when interpreting the dynamics.

\subsection{The two constraints and the spin-flop channel}
\label{sec:twoconstraints}
Write $Q_\pm=Q_A\pm Q_B$, so \eqref{eq:constraints} reads $Q_+=N$, $Q_-=0$. In Fourier
variables,
\begin{equation}
  Q_+=\sum_{\bk}|\bm S_{\bk}|^2,
  \qquad
  Q_-=\sum_j\epsilon_j|\bm\sigma_j|^2=\sum_{\bk}\bm S_{\bk}\cdot\bm S_{\bQ-\bk} .
  \label{eq:Qpm}
\end{equation}
$Q_+$ is diagonal; $Q_-$ \emph{couples $\bk$ to $\bQ-\bk$}.

Consider a homogeneous condensate ansatz in which the longitudinal component carries a
uniform amplitude $M$ and a staggered amplitude $m_s$, and the transverse component carries
a staggered amplitude $x$:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma^z_j=M+\epsilon_j m_s,\qquad \sigma^x_j=\epsilon_j x .
\end{equation}
(At this stage $M$, $m_s$, $x$ are simply parameters of the ansatz; $M$ is identified with
the uniform magnetisation in \eqref{eq:M} and $x$ with the order parameter in
\eqref{eq:xdef}.) Then, using $\epsilon_j^2=1$,
\begin{equation}
  |\bm\sigma_j|^2=M^2+x^2+m_s^2+2M\epsilon_jm_s
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  Q_-=2NMm_s ,
\end{equation}
so $M\neq0$ forces $m_s=0$: within this ansatz the separate constraints exclude a
longitudinal staggered component and select the transverse spin-flop channel. Transverse
order contributes $\epsilon_j^2x^2=x^2$ and is unaffected.

\FLAG{Scope.} This is an argument within the homogeneous-condensate ansatz, not a global
proof. A general state could in principle carry an unequal longitudinal mean together with
unequal fluctuation contributions on the two sublattices; excluding such a saddle requires
the full $(z_A,z_B)$ analysis including stability in the $z_-$ direction. We follow the
stable transverse branch identified in [K,\S IV] and record the stability question in

\subsection{Integral representation}
\label{sec:integralrep}
Represent each delta by a Bromwich integral,
$\delta(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}dz\,e^{-zy}$:
\begin{eqnarray}
  Z(\lambda)&=& 
  \int\prod_j d^2\bm\sigma_j\ e^{-\beta E_{\rm int}+h\sum_j\sigma^z_j} \underbrace{\oint\!\frac{dz_A\,dz_B}{(2\pi i)^2}
    e^{z_A(\frac{N}{2}-Q_A)+z_B(\frac{N}{2}-Q_B)}}_{\delta(\frac{N}{2}-Q_B)\delta(\frac{N}{2}-Q_A)} \nonumber\\
  &=&\oint\!\frac{dz_A\,dz_B}{(2\pi i)^2}\;e^{\frac N2(z_A+z_B)}\;
   \underbrace{\int\prod_j d^2\bm\sigma_j\,
    e^{-\beta E_{\rm int}+h\sum_j\sigma^z_j-z_AQ_A-z_BQ_B}}_{\displaystyle Z_z} .
  \label{eq:bromwich}
\end{eqnarray}

The spherical fields enter as \emph{saddle-point variables of a two-dimensional contour
integral}, not as physical fields; nothing about \eqref{eq:expfam} is compromised by using
them.

\subsection{Fourier transformation and sublattice decoupling}
To evaluate the integral $Z_z$, we transition to momentum space over the full lattice.
To this end we use the Fourier coordinates defined in Eq.(\ref{eq:ft}).

Because we are working on a simple cubic bipartite lattice, we can mathematically isolate the $A$ and $B$ sublattices using the antiferromagnetic ordering wavevector $\bQ = (\pi, \pi, \pi)$ (setting the lattice constant to 1). The phase factor $e^{i \bQ \cdot \vec r_j}$ evaluates to $+1$ for all sites $j \in A$ and $-1$ for all sites $j \in B$. 
We can therefore rewrite the sum of squares over an individual sublattice using this phase factor as a filter:
\begin{equation}
  Q_A = \sum_{j \in A} |\bm{\sigma}_j|^2 = \frac{1}{2} \sum_j |\bm{\sigma}_j|^2 \big(1 + e^{i \bQ \cdot \vec r_j}\big)
\end{equation}
and similarly $Q_B = \frac{1}{2} \sum_j |\bm{\sigma}_j|^2 \big(1 - e^{i \bQ \cdot \vec r_j}\big)$. 
Inserting these into the spherical constraint terms in $Z_z$, we obtain:
\begin{equation}
  z_A Q_A + z_B Q_B = \frac{1}{2}(z_A + z_B) \sum_j |\bm{\sigma}_j|^2 + \frac{1}{2}(z_A - z_B) \sum_j |\bm{\sigma}_j|^2 e^{i \bQ \cdot \vec r_j}.
\end{equation}
Applying the Fourier transform, the uniform sum of squares simply becomes
$\sum_{\bk}|\bm S_{\bk}|^2$ by Eq.(\ref{eq:parseval}). The alternating phase factor, however,
shifts the momentum by $\bQ$: the site sum now enforces $\bk'=-\bQ-\bk\equiv\bQ-\bk$ rather
than $\bk'=-\bk$, so it couples modes $\bk$ and $\bQ-\bk$:
\begin{equation}
  z_A Q_A + z_B Q_B = \frac{1}{2}(z_A + z_B) \sum_{\bk} |\bm{S}_{\bk}|^2
   + \frac{1}{2}(z_A - z_B) \sum_{\bk} \bm{S}_{\bk} \cdot \bm{S}_{\bQ-\bk},
\end{equation}
in agreement with \eqref{eq:Qpm}.

\FLAG{No conjugate on the second factor.} It is tempting to write
$\bm S_{\bk}\cdot\bm S^{*}_{\bQ-\bk}$, since that pairs $\bk$ with a mode of the same
momentum shift and is also real. It is a \emph{different} quantity:
$\bm S^{*}_{\bQ-\bk}=\bm S_{\bk-\bQ}$, and $\sum_{\bk}\bm S_{\bk}\cdot\bm S_{\bk-\bQ}$
replaces $2\,\mathrm{Re}(S_{\bq}^2)$ by $2|S_{\bq}|^2$ mode by mode. The manifestly
Hermitian way to write the correct object is
\begin{equation}
  Q_-=\sum_{\bk}\bm S^{*}_{\bk}\cdot\bm S_{\bk-\bQ}
     =\sum_{\bk}\bm S_{\bk}\cdot\bm S_{\bQ-\bk},
\end{equation}
the two being related by $\bk\to-\bk$ together with $\bm S_{-\bk}=\bm S^{*}_{\bk}$. This is
the form used to build the kernel \eqref{eq:twofieldkernel}.

The interaction energy translates cleanly into momentum space as well. Assuming translational invariance, the exponent $-\beta E_{\rm int} = -\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{i,j} \Jc_{ij} \bm{\sigma}_i \cdot \bm{\sigma}_j$ becomes $-\beta \sum_{\bk} \Jc(\bk) |\bm{S}_{\bk}|^2$. 
Finally, the uniform magnetic field couples strictly to the macroscopic zero-mode: $h \sum_j \sigma_j^z = h \sqrt{N} S_0^z$.

Combining these components, the full argument of the exponential in $Z_z$ becomes:
\begin{equation}
  -\sum_{\bk} \left[ \frac{1}{2}(z_A + z_B) + \beta \Jc(\bk) \right] |\bm{S}_{\bk}|^2 - \frac{1}{2}(z_A - z_B) \sum_{\bk} \bm{S}_{\bk} \cdot \bm{S}_{\bQ-\bk} + h \sqrt{N} S_0^z.
  \label{eq:fullexponent}
\end{equation}
This formulation exposes exactly how independent sublattice constraints ($z_A \neq z_B$) induce a mathematical mixing between orthogonal spatial modes.

\subsection{The Gaussian integral and the determinant}
On the symmetric saddle $z_A = z_B = z$ (justified in \S\ref{sec:zminus}), the mixing term $-\tfrac12(z_A-z_B)\bm{S}_{\bk}\cdot\bm{S}^*_{\bQ-\bk}$ from the sublattice decoupling vanishes entirely. This simplifies the problem dramatically, as the quadratic form becomes perfectly diagonal in momentum space:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ -\sum_{\bk}a_{\bk}|\bm S_{\bk}|^2+h\sqrt N\,S^z_0,
   \qquad a_{\bk}\equiv z+\beta\Jc(\bk)\ }
  \label{eq:quadform}
\end{equation}
with the soft mode located at the antiferromagnetic wavevector $\bk=\bQ$, where $a_{\bQ}=z-\beta P$. 

\subsubsection{Physical interpretation of the soft mode and condensation}
In the diagonalized quadratic form \eqref{eq:quadform}, the coefficient $a_{\bk} = z + \beta \Jc(\bk)$ represents the effective energy cost to thermally excite a spin fluctuation at wavevector $\bk$. Because the interaction is antiferromagnetic on a bipartite lattice, the absolute lowest-energy configuration is the perfectly staggered (alternating) pattern corresponding to the wavevector $\bk = \bQ$. At this mode, the exchange interaction reaches its most negative value, $\Jc(\bQ) = -P$, making $a_{\bQ} = z - \beta P$ the lowest-energy state in the spectrum.

For the Gaussian integrals over the continuous spin fields to remain convergent, the quadratic form must remain strictly negative definite, which imposes the mathematical boundary condition $z > \beta P$. As the system cools (increasing $\beta$), the constraint drives the \emph{gap} $r\equiv z-\beta P$ down towards zero.

\FLAG{$z$ itself increases.} It is the gap $r$ that shrinks, not $z$. Solving $\Wc(z)=1$ at
$H=0$ gives $z=1$ at $\beta=0$ (where the constraint reads $\int\!\dk\,a_{\bk}^{-1}=1$ with
$a_{\bk}\to z$) rising monotonically to $z=\beta_0P=W_{\rm sc}=1.516$ at criticality, while
$r$ falls from $1$ to $0$. Equivalently, the reduced field $\theta\equiv z/\beta$ decreases
to its lower bound $P$. Since the bound $\beta P$ itself moves with $\beta$, only $r$ (or
$\theta$) is a meaningful measure of the distance to criticality.

A continuous phase transition occurs at the temperature where $z$ reaches its moving lower bound, $z = \beta P$. Exactly at this boundary, the energetic cost of the staggered mode vanishes entirely ($a_{\bQ} = 0$), and the mode is said to "soften." Because the continuous integral over the remaining stiff modes can no longer account for the total normalized length of the spins, a macroscopic fraction of the spins condenses directly into this zero-energy $\bQ$-mode to balance the equation of state. In the planar spherical model, this mathematical condensation physically manifests as the emergence of the spontaneous transverse staggered magnetization, perfectly capturing the antiferromagnetic spin-flop transition.


To perform the integration over the continuous spin fields in $Z_z$, it is instructive to look at the corresponding quadratic form in real space. The exponent can be written using an $N \times N$ real, symmetric coupling matrix $A$, defined by its elements:
\begin{equation}
  A_{ij} = z\delta_{ij} + \frac{\beta}{2}\Jc_{ij}.
\end{equation}
(The factor of $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is required because the unrestricted double sum over sites $i$ and $j$ counts each interacting bond twice). 

Because the planar spins $\bm{\sigma}_i = (\sigma_i^x, \sigma_i^z)$ only interact via the dot product $\bm{\sigma}_i \cdot \bm{\sigma}_j = \sigma_i^x \sigma_j^x + \sigma_i^z \sigma_j^z$, the $x$ and $z$ components do not mix. Both components see the exact same coupling matrix $A$, but only the longitudinal component ($\sigma^z$) couples to the uniform magnetic field $h$. 

Let us group the spin components into two $N$-dimensional vectors: $u^x = (\sigma_1^x, \dots, \sigma_N^x)^\top$ and $u^z = (\sigma_1^z, \dots, \sigma_N^z)^\top$. The source vector for the magnetic field is $b = (h, h, \dots, h)^\top$ for the $z$-component, and strictly zero for the $x$-component. The full integral $Z_z$ then cleanly factors into the product of two multivariate Gaussian integrals:
\begin{equation}
  Z_z = \left[ \int d^N u^x \, e^{-(u^x)^\top A u^x} \right] \times \left[ \int d^N u^z \, e^{-(u^z)^\top A u^z + b^\top u^z} \right].
\end{equation}

We evaluate these using the standard multidimensional Gaussian integral identity:
\begin{equation}
  \int d^N u \, e^{-u^\top A u + b^\top u} = \pi^{N/2}(\det A)^{-1/2} e^{\frac{1}{4} b^\top A^{-1} b}.
\end{equation}

Applying this identity to our two components yields:
\begin{equation}
  Z_z = \left[ \pi^{N/2}(\det A)^{-1/2} \right] \times \left[ \pi^{N/2}(\det A)^{-1/2} e^{\frac{1}{4} b^\top A^{-1} b} \right] = \pi^N (\det A)^{-1} e^{\frac{1}{4} b^\top A^{-1} b}.
\end{equation}

Taking the natural logarithm of $Z_z$, we can break the result into two distinct physical contributions: the fluctuation determinant and the source shift.
\begin{equation}
  \ln Z_z = -\ln(\det A) + \frac{1}{4} b^\top A^{-1} b + N\ln\pi.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{1. The Determinant Term}
The determinant of a matrix is the product of its eigenvalues. Because $A$ is diagonalized by the Fourier transform, its eigenvalues are precisely the momentum-space coefficients $a_{\bk}$. Therefore, $\det A = \prod_{\bk} a_{\bk}$. Taking the logarithm converts this product into a sum over all modes:
\begin{equation}
  -\ln(\det A) = -\ln \left( \prod_{\bk} a_{\bk} \right) = -\sum_{\bk} \ln a_{\bk}.
\end{equation}
Notice that the power of $-1/2$ from the individual Gaussian integrals was perfectly cancelled by the fact that we have two independent spin components (planar spins, $n=2$), leaving a clean factor of $-1$.

\subsubsection*{2. The Source Term}
The source vector $b$ consists of a uniform field $h$ at every site. In momentum space, a uniform real-space vector corresponds purely to the macroscopic zero-mode ($\bk = \mathbf{0}$). Consequently, the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$ acting on $b$ only picks out the eigenvalue associated with the zero-mode, which is $a_0 = z + \beta \Jc(\mathbf{0})$. The quadratic product simplifies to:
\begin{equation}
  b^\top A^{-1} b = \sum_{i,j=1}^N h (A^{-1})_{ij} h = h^2 \sum_{i,j=1}^N (A^{-1})_{ij} = \frac{h^2 N}{a_0}.
\end{equation}

Combining the determinant evaluation and the zero-mode source term, we arrive at the exact expression for the unconstrained partition function logarithm:
\begin{equation}
  \ln Z_z = -\sum_{\bk}\ln a_{\bk} + \frac{h^2 N}{4a_0} + N\ln\pi .
  \label{eq:lnZ}
\end{equation}
\subsection{The saddle point}
\label{sec:saddle}
Combining \eqref{eq:bromwich}, \eqref{eq:lnZ} , we get
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \frac{\psi}{N}=\operatorname*{extr}_{z,x}\ \Phi,
  \qquad
  \Phi=z-\int\!\dk\,\ln\big[z+\beta\Jc(\bk)\big]
       +\frac{h^2}{4\big[z+\beta P\big]}
       -\big[z-\beta P\big]x^2 \;}
  \label{eq:Phi}
\end{equation}
up to a $\lambda$-independent constant, with $z\geq\beta P$ (the analogue of (K2.23)).

\FLAG{Which extremum.} ``$\operatorname{extr}$'' here means $\min_z\max_x$:
$\Phi_{zz}=I_2+\ldots>0$, so the $z$-saddle is a minimum along the real axis (as it must be
for a Bromwich contour deformed through it), while
$\partial^2\Phi/\partial x^2=-2(z-\beta P)\leq0$. The same sign convention makes
$\Phi_{--}>0$ in \S\ref{sec:zminus} the statement that the symmetric saddle is stable.

\subsubsection{The macroscopic condensate and the $x^2$ term}
\label{sec:condensate}
The term $-(z-\beta P)x^2$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi} is the fundamental mathematical mechanism for symmetry breaking in any spherical model in the thermodynamic limit.

When evaluating the Gaussian integral over the spin fields, transitioning from a discrete sum over $N$ wavevectors to a continuous integral $\int \dk$ intrinsically assigns zero statistical weight to any single discrete momentum mode. In the disordered phase ($z > \beta P$), this continuous approximation is perfectly sufficient to satisfy the macroscopic spherical constraint.

However, as the system cools into the ordered phase, the spherical field $z$ reaches its mathematical lower bound, $z = \beta P$. Exactly at this boundary, the energetic cost of the staggered mode $\bk = \bQ$ vanishes entirely. The continuous integral over the remaining modes is bounded and can no longer account for the total normalized length of the spins. To balance the equation of state, a macroscopic fraction of the spins must condense directly into this zero-energy mode. 

The variable $x$ represents the amplitude of this macroscopic occupation (which physically manifests as the transverse staggered magnetization). It must be explicitly separated from the bulk sum \emph{before} taking the thermodynamic limit. Furthermore, the extremization over $x$ is what flawlessly selects the thermodynamic phase:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial x} = -2\big[z - \beta P\big]x = 0
\end{equation}
In the disordered phase ($z > \beta P$), this condition strictly dictates $x = 0$. In the ordered phase, the continuous integral fails to satisfy the constraint, forcing the field to stick at $z = \beta P$. The bracketed term vanishes identically, allowing the condensate $x^2$ to become macroscopically populated to exactly make up the deficit in the spin length constraint.

\paragraph{Control of the saddle.} To do the saddle point approximation over $z$ (which ends in the maximization over $z$ in the Massieu potential), we have to make sure that the saddle point approximation is valid. To this end, we have to check $\Phi_{zz}$  , since it is the second derivative of the exponent function $\Phi$ with respect to the contour integration variable $z$. As such, $\Phi_{zz}$ measures the inverse variance (or sharpness) of the Gaussian peak in the complex plane, which mathematically justifies the saddle-point approximation. We have $\Phi_{zz}=I_2+O(1)$ with $I_2=\int\!\dk\,a_{\bk}^{-2}$, so the nominal Gaussian width of the $z$-contour is $\delta z\sim(N\Phi_{zz})^{-1/2}\sim N^{-1/2}r^{1/4}$, using $I_2\sim r^{-1/2}$, which follows from differentiating \eqref{eq:watson} below
(see also the definition of $r$ below). For a fixed distance $r>0$ from the boundary, this width vanishes as $N\to\infty$, making the saddle infinitely sharp and the evaluation exact.

\FLAG{But not uniformly near criticality.} The relevant comparison for the validity of the saddle-point method is not $\delta z$ itself, but $\delta z$ against the distance $r$ from the saddle to the branch point at $z=\beta P$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\delta z}{r}\sim N^{-1/2}r^{-3/4}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N r^{3/2}}},
  \qquad\text{so control requires}\qquad
  \boxed{\ N r^{3/2}\gg1\ }
\end{equation}
Since $r\sim\xi^{-2}$ and $N=L^D$ with $D=3$, this translates to $Nr^{3/2}\sim(L/\xi)^3\gg1$. Thus, the Gaussian saddle is uniform outside the finite-size critical window ($\xi\ll L$) and fails when the correlation length approaches the system size ($\xi\sim L$). The divergence of $\Phi_{zz}$ does \emph{not} by itself improve matters, since the higher derivatives diverge too and the saddle simultaneously approaches the edge of the convergence domain. The condensate construction of \S\ref{sec:condensate} is what supplies the proper thermodynamic-limit continuation through this finite-size critical region.

\subsection{The Spherical Mass and the Correlation-Length Exponent}
\label{sec:nu}

\subsubsection*{The Real-Space Correlation Length}
Near the phase transition, the lowest-energy thermal fluctuations occur around the antiferromagnetic ordering wavevector $\bk = \bQ$. For a small momentum deviation $\bq$ such that $\bk = \bQ - \bq$, we can Taylor expand the interaction. From our earlier definitions, the effective energy cost for these modes is (See also discussion around Eq.\ref{eq:smallq}):
$$a_{\bQ-\bq} = z + \beta \Jc(\bQ-\bq)$$

Using the small-$\bq$ expansion $\Jc(\bQ-\bq) \approx -P + \frac{\Jc}{2}q^2$, this becomes:
$$a_{\bQ-\bq} \approx (z - \beta P) + \frac{\beta \Jc}{2} q^2 \equiv r + c q^2$$
Here, we define $r \equiv z - \beta P \geq 0$ as the spherical mass (the energy gap required to excite the soft mode) and $c \equiv \beta \Jc / 2$ as the stiffness.

In momentum space, the continuous spin-spin correlation function is proportional to the inverse of the coupling matrix, meaning it scales as $1/a_{\bk}$. Near the soft mode, the propagator takes the classic Ornstein-Zernike form: $G(\bq) \propto 1 / (r + c q^2)$. When Fourier transformed into real space, the correlation function decays exponentially at large distances $R$ as $e^{-R/\xi} / R$. The characteristic length scale of this decay---the correlation length $\xi$---is determined exactly by the pole of the momentum-space propagator ($q^2 = -r/c$). This immediately identifies the correlation length as:
$$\xi = \sqrt{\frac{c}{r}} \implies \xi \sim r^{-1/2}$$

\subsubsection*{The Pitfall: $r$ is Not the Temperature}
Looking at $\xi \sim r^{-1/2}$, it is extremely tempting to conclude that the correlation length exponent is $\nu = 1/2$. However, this is incorrect. The critical exponent $\nu$ is defined with respect to the physical thermodynamic distance from the critical line (e.g., a reduced temperature parameter $\epsilon \propto T - T_c$), not the internal spherical mass $r$. To find the true exponent, we must determine exactly how the mathematical gap $r$ scales with the physical control parameter $\epsilon$ as we approach the phase boundary.

\subsubsection*{The Square-Root Singularity of the Watson Integral}
To link $r$ to the temperature, we must return to the saddle-point equation of state, which depends on the continuous momentum integral $\Wc(r) = \int \dk \frac{1}{r + c q^2 + \dots}$.

As the system cools toward the critical point, $r \to 0$. We can isolate the singular infrared behavior of this integral near the boundary by subtracting its value exactly at the critical point ($r=0$):
$$\Wc(r) - \Wc(0) = \int \dk \left[ \frac{1}{r + c q^2} - \frac{1}{c q^2} \right] + \mathcal{O}(r)$$

Combining the fractions inside the integral yields a single integrand. Transitioning to 3D spherical coordinates ($d^3q = 4\pi q^2 dq$) gives:
$$\Wc(r) - \Wc(0) = -\frac{r}{2\pi^2} \int_0^\infty \frac{q^2 \, dq}{c q^2 (r + c q^2)} = -\frac{r}{2\pi^2 c} \int_0^\infty \frac{dq}{r + c q^2}$$

This is a standard arctangent integral. Evaluating it from $0$ to $\infty$ yields exactly $\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{rc}}$. Multiplying this result by the prefactor reveals the crucial non-analytic cusp:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \Wc(r) = \Wc(0) - \frac{\sqrt{r}}{4\pi c^{3/2}} + \mathcal{O}(r)\ }
  \label{eq:watson}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Extracting the True Critical Exponent}
Let $\epsilon$ be a smooth, signed thermal scaling field normal to the finite-temperature critical line, defined such that $\epsilon > 0$ in the disordered phase and $\epsilon = 0$ exactly on the boundary. We can expand the full macroscopic saddle-point equation about this critical point.

Because the explicit temperature dependence (from the factors of $\beta$ in the constants) is completely smooth, it contributes a simple linear term $A_1 \epsilon$. However, the integral contributes the non-analytic $\sqrt{r}$ cusp we just derived. Setting the expansion to zero yields:
$$0 = A_1 \epsilon - C_1 \sqrt{r} + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2, r)$$
where $A_1$ and $C_1 = 1 / (4\pi c^{3/2})$ are strictly positive constants.

Rearranging this equation dictates that the square root of the spherical mass must scale linearly with the physical temperature deviation:
$$\sqrt{r} \sim \epsilon \implies r \sim \epsilon^2$$

Finally, we substitute this thermodynamic scaling back into our original definition of the correlation length:
$$\xi_+ \sim r^{-1/2} \sim (\epsilon^2)^{-1/2} = \epsilon^{-1}$$

By rigorously mapping the internal spherical mass to the physical temperature via the 3D momentum integral, we arrive at the exact correlation length critical exponent:
$$\nu = 1$$

Writing $\sqrt r\sim\epsilon$ with a signed $\epsilon$ would obscure the non-analyticity and
make the two sides of the line appear to have the same slope.


\paragraph{The $H=0$ tip is different.} With $M=h/2a_0$, the uniform field enters the
thermal scaling field \emph{quadratically} near the tip,
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon\ \sim\ \frac{T-T_0}{T_0}+\frac{H^2}{16P^2},
\end{equation}
so along $T=T_0$ one has $\epsilon\sim H^2$, $r\sim H^4$, $\xi\sim H^{-2}$. All statements
below about $\beta$ and $h$ projecting linearly onto the same thermal scaling field hold at
a \emph{generic} point with $H_c\neq0$, not at the tip.

\subsection{The zero-field critical temperature}
\label{sec:T0}
At $H=0$, $x=0$, $z=\beta_0P$, \eqref{eq:constraint} reads $\Wc=1$, i.e.
$\beta_0^{-1}\int\!\dk\,[P-\Jc(\bk)]^{-1}=1$. With
$P-\Jc(\bk)=3\Jc[1-\tfrac13\sum_\mu\cos k_\mu]$ this is $W_{\rm sc}/(3\Jc\beta_0)=1$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \kB T_0=\frac{3\Jc}{W_{\rm sc}}=3.9568\,J\ },
  \qquad
  \beta_0P=W_{\rm sc} .
\end{equation}
Converting with $\Jc=2J$ gives $W_3(\Jh(0))=2/\kB T_0$, which is (K2.29). 

\subsection{The critical line and the topology of the phase diagram}
On the boundary $x=0$ and $z=\beta P$ hold together, so $a_0=2\beta P$,
$M_c=h/4\beta P=\Hc_c/4P$, and $\Wc(\beta P)=\beta_0/\beta=t$ with $t\equiv T/T_0$. Then
\eqref{eq:constraint} reads $t+M_c^2=1$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \Hc_c(T)=4P\left(1-\frac{T}{T_0}\right)^{1/2}\ }
  \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
  T_c(\Hc)=T_0\left(1-\frac{\Hc^2}{16P^2}\right),
  \label{eq:boundary}
\end{equation}
which is (K2.39) at $\LK=1$. In control variables, with $u\equiv\beta P$,
\begin{equation}
  h_c(\beta)=4u\left(1-\frac{W_{\rm sc}}{u}\right)^{1/2}.
  \label{eq:boundary_bh}
\end{equation}
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{PRLrevisions/PhaseDiagram.pdf}
    \caption{The phase diagram of the model}
    \label{fig:PhaseDiagram}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Topology, stated carefully.} The ordered region is $|h|<h_c(\beta)$ for
$\beta>\beta_0$: an unbounded tongue, symmetric in $h\to-h$, beginning at
$(\beta_0,0)$ and extending to $\beta\to\infty$ (see Fig.\ref{fig:PhaseDiagram}). Two consequences, which an earlier draft
ran together:
\begin{itemize}
  \item States in \emph{different} phases can only be connected by crossing the critical
        boundary --- trivially, since they lie in different open sets.
  \item The disordered region is \textbf{connected}: two disordered points on opposite sides
        of the tongue can avoid the ordered phase by passing around its finite-temperature
        tip through $\beta<\beta_0$. What is impossible at finite controls is going around
        the low-temperature end, since the tongue extends to $\beta=\infty$.
\end{itemize}
So paths between two disordered states may either go around the tip or cut through the
ordered phase, and which is shorter is a quantitative question --- exactly the comparison
needed later. Topology alone settles nothing.

\section{The static covariance matrix}
\noindent
This section computes $\partial^2\psi/\partial\lambda^\mu\partial\lambda^\nu=\mathrm{Cov}(X_\mu,X_\nu)$,
the $t=0$ input to the dissipation metric. It is \emph{not} the metric itself, which
requires the time integration of \S4 --- see the caveat in \S\ref{sec:rank}.
\subsection{First derivatives}
By the envelope theorem (the saddle is stationary in $z$ and $x$), differentiate at fixed
$z,x$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{N}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial h}=\frac{h}{2a_0}=M ,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{N}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\beta}
   =-\left[\int\!\dk\,\frac{\Jc(\bk)}{a_{\bk}}+PM^2-Px^2\right]
   =-\frac{\avg{E_{\rm int}}}{N}.
\end{equation}
The Gaussian average of \eqref{eq:diag} is $\avg{|\bm S_{\bk}|^2}=1/a_{\bk}$ plus the
uniform condensate $NM^2$ at $\bk=0$ and the staggered condensate $Nx^2$ at $\bk=\bQ$; the
term $-Px^2$ is the energy of the macroscopic antiferromagnetic mode, since
$\Jc(\bQ)=-P$. \checkmark

\subsection{The ordered phase in closed form}
\label{sec:ordered}
In the ordered phase, $z=\beta P$, the $x^2$ term in \eqref{eq:Phi} has zero coefficient, and the order
parameter drops out of $\psi$. With $a_{\bk}=\beta[P+\Jc(\bk)]$ and $a_0=2\beta P$,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \frac{\psi}{N}\bigg|_{\rm ord}=\beta P-\ln\beta+\frac{h^2}{8\beta P}+\mathrm{const}\ }
  \label{eq:psiord}
\end{equation}
elementary and analytic in $(\beta,h)$ throughout the ordered region. By \eqref{eq:expfam},
the \emph{thermodynamic-limit covariance densities} are
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{Var}(X_1)}{N}=\frac{1}{\beta^2}+\frac{h^2}{4\beta^3P},
  \quad
  \frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X_1,X_2)}{N}=-\frac{h}{4\beta^2P},
  \quad
  \frac{\mathrm{Var}(X_2)}{N}=\frac{1}{4\beta P},
  \label{eq:ordhess}
\end{equation}
all finite. 

\subsection{Decoupling of the $z_-$ sector}
\label{sec:zminus}
The representation \eqref{eq:bromwich} carries two contour variables. While the single-field Massieu potential perfectly describes the final equilibrium state, we cannot rigorously assume that the two sublattices fluctuate identically ($z_A = z_B$) without proof—especially since local symmetry breaking is a hallmark of antiferromagnetic systems. 

Write
\begin{equation}
  z_+=\tfrac12(z_A+z_B),\qquad z_-=\tfrac12(z_A-z_B).
\end{equation}
The two-field potential, $\Phi(z_+, z_-)$, explicitly maps the complete, unrestricted mathematical landscape of the contour integral before the system settles into equilibrium. By evaluating this full landscape, we can calculate the second derivative (curvature) with respect to the antisymmetric field difference, $z_-$. Proving that this curvature is strictly positive ($\Phi_{--} > 0$) provides the crucial mathematical guarantee that the symmetric saddle point is a true, stable minimum for the thermodynamic fluctuations, rather than an unstable ridge or an artificial mathematical assumption. To this end, let us calculate $\Phi(z_-,z_+)$. 

At $H'_\perp=0$ both controls $\lambda=(\beta,h)$ are sublattice-symmetric, and sublattice
exchange $A\leftrightarrow B$ maps $z_-\to-z_-$ while leaving everything else fixed, so
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(\lambda,z_+,z_-)=\Phi(\lambda,z_+,-z_-):
\end{equation}
$\Phi$ is \emph{even} in $z_-$. Consequently, at $z_-=0$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z_-}=0
  \quad\text{(a symmetric saddle exists)},
  \qquad
  \Phi_{+-}=0,
  \qquad
  \Phi_{\mu-}=0\ \ (\mu=\beta,h),
\end{equation}
all being odd functions evaluated at zero. The general pullback Hessian
\begin{equation}
  \bar\psi_{\mu\nu}=\Phi_{\mu\nu}-\Phi_{\mu a}\big(\Phi_{ab}\big)^{-1}\Phi_{b\nu},
  \qquad a,b\in\{+,-\},
  \label{eq:fullschur}
\end{equation}
therefore block-diagonalises: the $z_-$ sector drops out entirely and \eqref{eq:fullschur}
collapses to the one-variable Schur complement \eqref{eq:hessian}. This justifies using a
single spherical field \emph{for the thermodynamic-limit symmetric branch and for
derivatives with respect to the symmetric controls} $(\beta,h)$ --- not in general, and not
for the dynamics, where $Q_-$ is retained (\S\ref{sec:twoconstraints}, \S\ref{sec:qmode}).

Evenness guarantees only that a symmetric saddle \emph{exists}; it does not by itself
exclude a competing branch with $z_A\neq z_B$. That can be checked directly.

\paragraph{The two-field potential.} With $\epsilon_j=+1$ on $A$, the spherical term of
\eqref{eq:bromwich} is $-z_AQ_A-z_BQ_B=-z_+Q_+-z_-Q_-$, so the positive-definite form is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal Q=\sum_{\bk}\big[z_++\beta\Jc(\bk)\big]|\bm S_{\bk}|^2
   \;+\;z_-\sum_{\bk}\bm S^{*}_{\bk}\cdot\bm S_{\bk-\bQ} .
\end{equation}
Since $2\bQ\equiv0$, the map $\bk\mapsto\bk-\bQ$ is an involution without fixed points and
the $N$ wavevectors split into $N/2$ pairs. Writing $u=\bm S_{\bk}$, $v=\bm S_{\bk-\bQ}$,
the diagonal gives $a_{\bk}|u|^2+a_{\bk-\bQ}|v|^2$ with $\Jc(\bk-\bQ)=-\Jc(\bk)$ by
\eqref{eq:bipartite}, while the $z_-$ term contributes from the indices $\bk$ and $\bk-\bQ$
as $z_-(u^{*}\!\cdot v+v^{*}\!\cdot u)$. Hence
\begin{equation}
  K_{\bk}=\begin{pmatrix} z_++\beta\Jc(\bk) & \;\;z_-\\ \;\;z_- & z_+-\beta\Jc(\bk)\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \det K_{\bk} = z_+^2-z_-^2-\beta^2\Jc(\bk)^2 .
  \label{eq:twofieldkernel}
\end{equation}


Hence
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(z_+,z_-)=z_+-\frac12\int\!\dk\,\ln\!\big[z_+^2-z_-^2-\beta^2\Jc(\bk)^2\big]
   +\frac{h^2}{4}\,\frac{z_+-\beta P}{z_+^2-z_-^2-\beta^2P^2},
  \label{eq:twofield}
\end{equation}
the field term being the $(0,0)$ entry of $K^{-1}$ on the $(\bk{=}0,\bQ)$ pair, i.e.\ the
$(1,1)$ cofactor $(z_+-\beta P)$ over $\det K_0$.

\FLAG{Evenness is manifest.} $z_-$ appears in \eqref{eq:twofield} only as $z_-^2$, so
$\Phi(z_+,z_-)=\Phi(z_+,-z_-)$ can be read off directly. The sublattice-exchange argument of
\S\ref{sec:zminus} is then a check on the algebra rather than the sole justification for
$\Phi_{\mu-}=\Phi_{+-}=0$; conversely, the explicit form is what lets one evaluate
$\Phi_{--}$ and settle stability, which symmetry alone cannot do.

At $z_-=0$ the bipartite identity recovers \eqref{eq:Phi}:
$-\tfrac12\int\ln[(z+\beta\Jc)(z-\beta\Jc)]=-\int\ln(z+\beta\Jc)$ after $\bk\to\bQ-\bk$ in
the second factor.

\paragraph{Disordered branch: stable.} Differentiating \eqref{eq:twofield} twice,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \Phi_{--}\big|_{z_-=0}
   =\int\!\dk\,\frac{1}{z^2-\beta^2\Jc(\bk)^2}
   +\frac{h^2}{2(z-\beta P)(z+\beta P)^2}\ }
\end{equation}
which is manifestly positive for $z>\beta P$. For $h\neq0$ the second term diverges
\emph{positively} as $r^{-1}$ on approach to the critical line, so the symmetric saddle is
locally stable on the disordered branch and becomes increasingly so near criticality.

\paragraph{Ordered branch: forced, not merely stable.} The Gaussian convergence domain is
$z_+^2-z_-^2\geq\beta^2P^2$. Since the ordered saddle has $z_+=\beta P$, this forces
\begin{equation}
  z_-=0 .
\end{equation}
The ordered solution therefore sits at the \emph{boundary} of the allowed $z_-$ domain, and
is not a regular interior saddle for which one would evaluate $\Phi_{--}$ at all. This is
stronger than a curvature check and removes the ambiguity.

\subsection{The longitudinal $\bQ$-mode}
\label{sec:qmode}
One structural point must be made explicit. The determinant $-\sum_{\bk}\ln a_{\bk}$ runs
over \emph{both} spin components, so excising $\bk=\bQ$  removes both
the transverse and the longitudinal zero modes. Only the transverse one is then restored, as
$S^x_{\bQ}=\sqrt N x$.

The longitudinal mode $S^z_{\bQ}$ is not restored, and should not be: it is not an
independent Gaussian zero mode, because fluctuations in it violate the sublattice-difference
constraint unless compensated elsewhere. This is \S\ref{sec:twoconstraints} in Fourier
language --- $S^z_{\bQ}\propto\sqrt N m_s$ and $Q_-=2NMm_s$.
For $H\neq 0$, the longitudinal staggered mode is not an independent Gaussian zero mode: at linear order the constraint $Q_-=0$ slaves it to the remaining modes. At $H=0$, where $M=0$ and the full $O(2)$ symmetry is restored, this argument no longer applies; the second zero mode represents a global rotation of the condensate and must be treated separately. The $H=0$ axis will therefore require a separate limiting analysis in the dynamics.

\FLAG{Harmless here, essential later.} For the Massieu-potential \emph{density} the omission
changes only subextensive terms, so nothing in \S2 is affected. For the dynamics it is
decisive: the longitudinal $\bQ$-mode must be regularised through the $Q_-$ projection
rather than simply discarded, since a projected Langevin process has to evolve it somehow.
Together with the constraint-slaving of $\delta S^x_{\bQ}$ noted in \S\ref{sec:cost}, this
is the main reason \S4 cannot be assembled from independent Ornstein--Uhlenbeck modes.

\subsection{Second derivatives on the disordered side}
On the disordered side $z_*$ moves with $\lambda$, so
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\
  \frac{1}{N}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial\lambda^\mu\partial\lambda^\nu}
  =\frac{\partial^2\Phi}{\partial\lambda^\mu\partial\lambda^\nu}\bigg|_z
   -\frac{\Phi_{\mu z}\,\Phi_{z\nu}}{\Phi_{zz}} \ }
  \label{eq:hessian}
\end{equation}
where $z\equiv z_+$ by \S\ref{sec:zminus}. This is purely a statement about evaluating a
contour integral by saddle point --- how the stationary point moves with $\lambda$ --- and
carries no ensemble caveat, \eqref{eq:expfam} being exact independently.

With $I_n\equiv\int\!\dk\,a_{\bk}^{-n}$ ($I_0=1$, $I_1=\Wc$),
\begin{align}
  \Phi_{zz}&=I_2+\frac{h^2}{2a_0^3},
  &\Phi_{\beta z}&=\int\!\dk\,\frac{\Jc}{a^2}+\frac{h^2P}{2a_0^3},
  &\Phi_{\beta\beta}\big|_z&=\int\!\dk\,\frac{\Jc^2}{a^2}+\frac{h^2P^2}{2a_0^3},\nonumber\\
  \Phi_{hz}&=-\frac{h}{2a_0^2},
  &\Phi_{h\beta}&=-\frac{hP}{2a_0^2},
  &\Phi_{hh}\big|_z&=\frac{1}{2a_0}.
\end{align}

\subsection{The cancellation}
\label{sec:cancel}
As $r\to0$, $I_2\sim(8\pi c^{3/2}\sqrt r)^{-1}\to\infty$ while $I_1=\Wc$ stays finite.
Eliminate $\Jc$ in favour of $a$: since $a_{\bk}=r+\beta[P+\Jc(\bk)]$,
\begin{equation}
  \Jc(\bk)=-P+\frac{a_{\bk}-r}{\beta}
  \ \Longrightarrow\
  \int\!\dk\,\frac{\Jc}{a^2}=-PI_2+\frac{A}{\beta},
  \quad
  \int\!\dk\,\frac{\Jc^2}{a^2}=P^2I_2-\frac{2PA}{\beta}+\frac{C}{\beta^2},
\end{equation}
with $A\equiv I_1-rI_2$, $C\equiv I_0-2rI_1+r^2I_2$. Writing $R\equiv h^2/2a_0^3$,
$D_z\equiv I_2+R$, $B\equiv A/\beta+2PR$, the $P^2D_z$ terms cancel identically:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{N}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial\beta^2}
  =P^2D_z-\frac{2PA}{\beta}+\frac{C}{\beta^2}-\frac{(-PD_z+B)^2}{D_z}
   =\frac{C}{\beta^2}+4P^2R-\frac{B^2}{D_z} .
  \label{eq:cancelled}
\end{equation}
The mechanism: near $\bQ$ the coupling $\Jc(\bk)\to-P$ is constant, so the singular part of
$\Phi_{\beta z}$ is a fixed multiple of that of $\Phi_{zz}$.

\FLAG{Finite, not analytic.} $A$ and $B$ are \emph{finite} as $r\to0$ but carry
$O(\sqrt r)$ cusps:  $I_1=\Wc(0)-\sqrt r/4\pi c^{3/2}$ and
$rI_2=\sqrt r/8\pi c^{3/2}$, so $A=\Wc(0)-3\sqrt r/8\pi c^{3/2}+O(r)$. ``Regular'' in an
earlier draft should read ``finite''. Similarly $C\to I_0=1$.

Taking $r\to0$: $C\to1$, $B^2/D_z\to0$, $4P^2R\to h^2/4\beta^3P$, recovering
\eqref{eq:ordhess} exactly, and likewise for the other two components.  The
Hessian is finite \emph{and continuous} across the critical line, computed independently
from the two sides.

The leading non-analytic correction is
\begin{equation}
  -\frac{B^2}{D_z}\ \sim\ -\,\mathrm{const}\cdot\sqrt r\ \sim\ -\,\mathrm{const}\cdot|\epsilon| ,
\end{equation}
nonzero at a generic finite-temperature point (its amplitude can vanish at special
endpoints). This is precisely the $\alpha=-1$ cusp, $|\epsilon|^{-\alpha}=|\epsilon|$ ---
obtained from the cancellation rather than assumed. Note it is a cusp, not a kink: the
modulus is essential.
On the disordered side, the leading Schur-complement correction is proportional to $-\epsilon$ as $\epsilon \to 0^+$. When the two thermodynamic branches are matched and their analytic backgrounds are separated, this produces a term proportional to $|\epsilon|$, as expected for $\alpha=-1$.

\subsection{Rank}
\label{sec:rank}
From \eqref{eq:ordhess},
\begin{equation}
  \det\left[\frac{1}{N}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial\lambda\partial\lambda}\right]
  =\frac{1}{4\beta^3P}>0 :
\end{equation}
the static covariance (Fisher information) is full rank. The corresponding
object in the mean field antiferromagnetic model we used earlier is rank one with $\det g=0$, and the optimum collapses onto lines of constant $m$;
that degeneracy is absent here.

\FLAG{Not yet a statement about the metric.} Full rank of the static covariance does not by
itself establish that the time-integrated friction tensor
$g_{\mu\nu}=\avg{\delta X_\mu,(-\mathcal L)^{-1}\delta X_\nu}$ has no null direction. For an
ergodic detailed-balance process one expects positive definiteness whenever no nonzero
combination $c^\mu X_\mu$ is constant, but that conclusion uses the dynamics and is to be
verified in \S4.

\subsection{Critical exponents}
\label{sec:crit}
Along the finite-temperature critical line, exactly,
\begin{equation}
  \alpha=-1,\quad \beta_{\rm op}=\tfrac12,\quad \gamma=2,\quad \delta=5,\quad
  \nu=1,\quad \eta=0 ,
\end{equation}
with $\nu=1$ now derived in \S\ref{sec:nu}. In the notation of [BR, Eq.~(5)],
\begin{equation}
  b=D\nu=2-\alpha=3,\qquad
  \Delta=\beta_{\rm op}\delta=\tfrac52,\qquad
  p=\frac b\Delta=1+\frac1\delta=\frac65,\qquad
  \frac{z_{\rm dyn}}{D}=\frac23 ,
\end{equation}
and the convergence condition of [BR] holds exactly:
\begin{equation}
  b\Big(1-\frac{z_{\rm dyn}}{D}\Big)=1>0 .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Scope of the universality statement.} These exponents govern the
\emph{finite-temperature} critical line. The $T=0$, $\Hc=4P$ endpoint is not described by
this scaling.

\paragraph{$p$ does not describe this control manifold.} The Widom variable $h/t^\Delta$ is
built from the field conjugate to the order parameter, which here is $\Hc'_\perp$, and
$\Hc'_\perp=0$ throughout. At a \emph{generic} point of the critical line ($H_c\neq0$),
variations of $\beta$ and of the uniform field merely have different linear projections onto
the same thermal scaling field normal to the line, and the tangential variation is
non-singular at leading order; at the $H=0$ tip the uniform field enters quadratically
(\S\ref{sec:nu}). Either way, $p=6/5$ remains correct universality-class data but describes
an approach this slice cannot realise; the earlier claim that $\kappa=pz_{\rm dyn}/D=4/5$
applies ``in the field direction'' is withdrawn. This is also the scaling-language reason to
expect the mildness found in \S4: the control parameters never couple to the ordering field.

\paragraph{Structural point.} Sections \ref{sec:ordered} and \ref{sec:cancel} establish that
the static Hessian does not diverge at the transition, consistent with $\alpha=-1<0$ and
with [K]'s statement (below K2.60) that $\chi$, $\chi'$ and $C_v$ remain finite and
continuous across the boundary for $H\neq0$. Together with \eqref{eq:decoupling}, any
divergence of $g_{\mu\nu}$ must come entirely from the time integration.

\section{Dynamics}
\noindent
Notation reminder: $\lambda=(\beta,h)$, $X_1=-E_{\rm int}$,
$X_2=M_{\rm tot}$, $a_{\bk}=z+\beta\Jc(\bk)$, $r=z-\beta P$, $c=\beta\Jc/2$, $n=2$
components, and $I_p\equiv\int\!\dk\,a_{\bk}^{-p}$ with $I_1=\Wc$.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Result of this section.} Define the time-integrated covariance
\begin{equation}
  \Tm_{\mu\nu}\equiv\int_0^\infty\!\avg{\delta X_\mu(t)\,\delta X_\nu(0)}\,dt ,
  \label{eq:Tdef}
\end{equation}
which is \emph{not} the manuscript's metric --- the conversion is a scalar factor discussed
in \S\ref{sec:objective}. For the $h=0$ disordered branch, in the thermodynamic limit taken
\emph{before} the critical limit, the projected Model~A dynamics gives, per site,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \Tm_{11}=\frac{1}{4\Gamma\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{I_3}{I_2^{2}}-1\right)
   =\frac{1}{4\Gamma\beta^{2}}\;
    \frac{\mathrm{Var}_\rho\!\big(a^{-1}\big)}{\avg{a^{-1}}_\rho^{2}}\ }
  \label{eq:T11}
\end{equation}
with $d\rho=a_{\bk}^{-1}\dk$ a probability measure (normalised, since $I_1=1$ there). It
diverges as
\begin{equation}
  \frac{I_3}{I_2^2}\ \simeq\ 2\pi c^{3/2}\,r^{-1/2}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \Tm_{11}\ \sim\ r^{-1/2}\ \sim\ |\epsilon|^{-1} ,
\end{equation}
using $r\sim\epsilon^2$ from \S2.5 of the \S0--\S3 notes. The conversion to the manuscript's
$g$ is smooth and $\beta_c$ is finite, so the exponent survives:
$\sqrt g\sim|\epsilon|^{-1/2}$, integrable.

\FLAG{What this does and does not establish.} It establishes that the thermodynamic
\emph{distance from the disordered phase to the critical line} is finite, at the $h=0$ tip.
It does \emph{not} establish a finite distance \emph{across} the transition: the ordered
side, where $r=0$ identically and the spectrum is massless throughout, is not computed here.
Nor does it cover a generic boundary point $h\neq0$. See \S\ref{sec:remaining}.

\section{Projected dynamics and the time-integrated covariance}
%===============================================================

\subsection{The process}
The state space is $\{Q_+=N,\;Q_-=0\}$, a product of two spheres. Write
\begin{equation}
  \Uc=\beta E_{\rm int}-h\sum_j\sigma^z_j
\end{equation}
for the dimensionless dynamical Hamiltonian, so the equilibrium measure of \S1.1 is
$\propto e^{-\Uc}d\nu$. Introduce $\Em=\mathrm{diag}(\epsilon_j)\otimes\mathsf I_n$, so that
$Q_+=\bs^\top\bs$ and $Q_-=\bs^\top\Em\bs$. The two constraint normals are
$\nabla Q_+=2\bs$ and $\nabla Q_-=2\Em\bs$; they are orthogonal on the manifold, and the
tangent projector is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \Pt=\mathsf I-\frac{\bs\bs^\top}{N}-\frac{(\Em\bs)(\Em\bs)^\top}{N}\ }
  \label{eq:projector}
\end{equation}
The process is the projected overdamped Langevin equation, written in Stratonovich form so
that no It\^o drift is left implicit:
\begin{equation}
  d\bs=-\Gamma\,\Pt\nabla\Uc\,dt+\sqrt{2\Gamma}\,\Pt\circ d\mathsf W .
  \label{eq:sde}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Why Model A, and what is and is not a choice.} The A/B classification refers to
whether the \emph{order parameter} obeys a local conservation law. Model~B is excluded
because driving $h$ must change $M_{\rm tot}$ ($\partial\psi/\partial h=\avg{M_{\rm tot}}$).
The spherical constraints are a different kind of object: $Q_\pm$ are quadratic in the spins
and define the state space rather than a hydrodynamic density, so ``conserving them'' is not
a Model letter --- any admissible dynamics must keep the velocity tangent to the manifold.
What \emph{is} a choice is the mobility: \eqref{eq:sde} takes it to be $\Gamma\Pt$, the
simplest positive tangential operator. Other positive mobilities satisfy detailed balance
with the same equilibrium measure; they should share long-wavelength universality but not
prefactors or geometry. (An earlier draft said ``there is no third option'', which
overstates this.)

\subsection{Generator and Dirichlet form}
\label{sec:generator}
Rather than work with \eqref{eq:sde}, define the process by its generator, which is fixed by
the equilibrium measure and the mobility alone:
\begin{equation}
  \Lc f=\Gamma\Big[\Delta_M f-\nabla_M\Uc\cdot\nabla_M f\Big],
  \qquad
  \avg{f(-\Lc)g}=\Gamma\avg{\nabla_M f\cdot\nabla_M g},
  \label{eq:dirichlet}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta_M,\nabla_M$ the Laplace--Beltrami operator and gradient on the manifold. This
is the least ambiguous route: it has the restricted Gibbs measure as invariant distribution
by construction, and the stochastic-calculus convention never appears. Then
\begin{equation}
  \Tm_{\mu\nu}=\avg{\delta X_\mu\,(-\Lc)^{-1}\delta X_\nu}
   =\avg{\delta X_\mu\,\phi_\nu}
   =\Gamma\avg{\nabla_M\phi_\mu\cdot\nabla_M\phi_\nu},
  \qquad -\Lc\phi_\mu=\delta X_\mu ,
  \label{eq:twoforms}
\end{equation}
and we evaluate the last form, which requires no conditional-covariance formula.

\subsection{Reduction to the $Q_+$ sector at $h=0$}
\label{sec:reduction}
Sections \ref{sec:quad}--\ref{sec:crit4} use only the first subtraction in
\eqref{eq:projector}, i.e.\ the single sphere $Q_+=N$. That is not literally the strict
model, and the reduction needs an argument rather than an appeal to symmetry.

Write $\mathsf A=z\mathsf I+\mathsf K$, where $\mathsf K$ carries the bipartite interaction.
Because $\mathsf K$ connects opposite sublattices only,
\begin{equation}
  \{\mathsf K,\Em\}=0
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \{\mathsf A,\Em\}=2z\Em ,
\end{equation}
and therefore, for any configuration on the constraint manifold,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \bs^\top\mathsf A\Em\bs=\tfrac12\bs^\top\{\mathsf A,\Em\}\bs=z\,Q_-=0\ }
  \label{eq:anticomm}
\end{equation}
(the antisymmetric part of $\mathsf A\Em$ drops from a quadratic form). Since at $h=0$ the
drift is $\nabla\Uc=2\mathsf A\bs$, its component along the $Q_-$ normal is
$(\Em\bs)^\top(2\mathsf A\bs)=2zQ_-=0$ \emph{exactly}. The deterministic dynamics therefore
never pushes along $Q_-$, and the residual difference between the one- and two-sphere
processes comes only from the Laplace--Beltrami part, which for symmetric quadratic
observables contributes subleading terms at fixed $r>0$.

\FLAG{Scope.} \eqref{eq:anticomm} fails once $h\neq0$: the source contributes
$(\Em\bs)^\top(-h\vec u_z)=-h\sqrt N\,S^z_{\bQ}\neq0$. And in the ordered phase the
condensate makes $Q_-$ essential (\S3.4 of the \S0--\S3 notes). A previous draft said
``rank two once $Q_-$ is included''; more precisely, at $h=0$ the second constraint produces
no additional \emph{leading} rank-one correction in the symmetric quadratic sector --- its
effect is subextensive --- whereas at $h\neq0$ and in the ordered phase it is genuinely
leading.

\subsection{Action on linear and quadratic observables}
\label{sec:quad}
Regard the configuration as $\bs\in\mathbb R^{m}$, $m=nN$, with $|\bs|^2=N$, so the sphere
has radius $R=\sqrt N$. With $\mathsf A_{jj'}=z\delta_{jj'}+\tfrac\beta2\Jc_{jj'}$
(eigenvalues $a_{\bk}$), on the constraint surface
$\Uc=\bs^\top\mathsf A\bs-zN-h\sqrt N S^z_0\equiv\Ac-zN-h\sqrt N S^z_0$.

For $F$ homogeneous of degree $d$,
$\Delta_MF=\nabla^2F-\partial_r^2F-\frac{m-1}{r}\partial_rF$ at $r=R$, giving
\begin{align}
  \text{degree 1:}\quad &\Delta_M F=-\tfrac{m-1}{N}F\simeq-nF,\\
  \text{degree 2, } F=\bs^\top\mathsf B\bs:\quad &\Delta_M F=2n\,\mathrm{tr}\mathsf B-2nF ,
\end{align}
while the drift term, at $h=0$ with $P_+=\mathsf I-\bs\bs^\top/N$, is
$\nabla\Uc\cdot P_+\nabla F=4[\bs^\top\mathsf A\mathsf B\bs-\tfrac1N\Ac F]$.

\paragraph{Curvature cancels the mean constraint force.} Since
$\avg{\Ac}=\tfrac n2\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf A\mathsf A^{-1})=\tfrac{nN}{2}$, we have
$\tfrac4N\Ac F=2nF+\tfrac4N\delta\Ac\,F$, and the $2nF$ cancels the $-2nF$ from $\Delta_M$
(likewise $nF$ against $-nF$ at degree 1). Only the \emph{fluctuation} of the constraint
force survives. Retaining $\tfrac4N\avg F\,\delta\Ac$ and centring,
\begin{equation}
  -\Lc\,\delta\!\left(\bs^\top\mathsf B\bs\right)
   =4\Gamma\,\delta\!\left(\bs^\top\mathsf A\mathsf B\bs\right)
    -\frac{4\Gamma\avg{F}}{N}\,\delta\Ac
    \;-\;\frac{4\Gamma}{N}\Big[\delta\Ac\,\delta F-\avg{\delta\Ac\,\delta F}\Big]
    \;+\;\ldots
  \label{eq:Mop}
\end{equation}
\FLAG{The last term is dropped, and the condition is the familiar one.} At fixed $r>0$,
$\delta\Ac=O(\sqrt N)$ and $\delta F=O(\sqrt N)$, so the nonlinear term is $O(1)$ against
$O(\sqrt N)$ retained: the linear closure is correct at leading thermodynamic order. Near
criticality it must be power-counted for the observable actually used,
$\Gc=\delta(\bs^\top\mathsf A^{-1}\bs)$. With $\mathrm{Var}(\Gc)=\tfrac n2NI_4$ and
$I_4\sim r^{-5/2}$,
\begin{equation}
  \delta\Gc\sim\sqrt{NI_4}\sim\sqrt N\,r^{-5/4},
  \qquad
  \underbrace{4\Gamma I_2\,\delta\Ac}_{\text{retained}}\sim\sqrt N\,r^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  \underbrace{\tfrac{4\Gamma}{N}\delta\Ac\,\delta\Gc}_{\text{dropped}}\sim r^{-5/4},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \frac{\text{dropped}}{\text{retained}}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{N r^{3/2}}}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \text{controlled for } Nr^{3/2}\gg1,\ \text{i.e. }\xi\ll L\ }
  \label{eq:control}
\end{equation}
This is \emph{the same} condition that controls the static saddle point (\S2.3 of the
\S0--\S3 notes). With the conventional order of limits --- $N\to\infty$ first, then
$r\to0$ --- the nonlinear term drops and the exponent below is correct. It is not a uniform
finite-size result and it is not exact at the critical point.

Dropping the nonlinear term and writing $\delta F=\sum_{\bk}b_{\bk}y_{\bk}$ with
$y_{\bk}=\delta|\bm S_{\bk}|^2$,
\begin{equation}
  \Mc\,\vec b\;\equiv\;\text{(coefficients of }-\Lc\delta F)\;=\;
  4\Gamma\Big[\vec a\odot\vec b-\frac{n}{2N}\big(\vec b\cdot\vec a^{-1}\big)\,\vec a\Big] :
  \label{eq:Mmatrix}
\end{equation}
diagonal plus rank one. Without the second term this is the ordinary statement that
$y_{\bk}$ relaxes at $4\Gamma a_{\bk}$.

\subsection{The kernel is a redundancy, not a zero mode}
\label{sec:kernel}
Taking $\vec b=\vec 1$,
\begin{equation}
  \Mc\vec1=4\Gamma\vec a\big[1-\tfrac n2 I_1\big]=0 ,
\end{equation}
because the spherical constraint is exactly $\tfrac n2I_1=1$ (at $h=0$, $x=0$). This is a
check on \eqref{eq:Mmatrix}: $\vec b=\vec1$ represents $\delta Q_+$, which is
\emph{identically zero} on the manifold. The apparent kernel is the redundancy of
parametrising functions by $\vec b$; all equations below are read modulo $\vec1$.

\paragraph{Why the unconstrained calculation fails.} Under the unconstrained generator,
$\Lc_0y_{\bk}=-4\Gamma a_{\bk}y_{\bk}$, so
\begin{equation}
  (-\Lc_0)^{-1}\delta\Ac=\sum_{\bk}\frac{a_{\bk}}{4\Gamma a_{\bk}}y_{\bk}
   =\frac{1}{4\Gamma}\,\delta Q_+ .
\end{equation}
The weights $a_{\bk}$ of the energy-weight identity cancel the relaxation rates
\emph{exactly}, so the unconstrained resolvent maps $\delta E_{\rm int}$ onto $\delta Q_+$,
which vanishes on the constraint surface. The earlier draft evaluated
$\avg{\delta\Ac(-\Lc_0)^{-1}\delta\Ac}=\tfrac{nN}{8\Gamma}I_1$ and read it as the metric; it
is the coefficient of a quantity that does not fluctuate. The whole answer comes from the
constraint term of \eqref{eq:Mmatrix}, not from a correction to it.

\subsection{Solving the resolvent equation}
Solve $\Mc\vec\phi=\vec a+\kappa\vec1$ (the $\kappa\vec1$ being the freedom of
\S\ref{sec:kernel}). With $c_\phi\equiv\tfrac1N\vec\phi\cdot\vec a^{-1}$ and $n=2$,
\begin{equation}
  4\Gamma\big[a_{\bk}\phi_{\bk}-c_\phi a_{\bk}\big]=a_{\bk}+\kappa
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \phi_{\bk}=\Big(c_\phi+\tfrac{1}{4\Gamma}\Big)+\frac{\kappa}{4\Gamma a_{\bk}} ,
\end{equation}
and self-consistency with $I_1=1$ gives $\kappa=-1/I_2$. Hence, up to an additive constant,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \phi_{\Ac}\equiv(-\Lc)^{-1}\delta\Ac=-\frac{\Gc}{4\Gamma I_2},
   \qquad \Gc\equiv\delta\big(\bs^\top\mathsf A^{-1}\bs\big)=\sum_{\bk}\frac{y_{\bk}}{a_{\bk}}\ }
  \label{eq:phiA}
\end{equation}
equivalently the operator identity $-\Lc\,\Gc=-4\Gamma I_2\,\delta\Ac$.

\FLAG{Do not confuse $\phi_{\Ac}$ with $\phi_1$.} The source for the $\beta$-coordinate is
$\delta X_1=-\delta E_{\rm int}=-\beta^{-1}\delta\Ac$, not $+\delta\Ac$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \phi_1\equiv(-\Lc)^{-1}\delta X_1=-\frac1\beta\phi_{\Ac}
   =+\frac{\Gc}{4\Gamma\beta I_2}\ }
  \label{eq:phi1}
\end{equation}
An earlier draft quoted $\phi_{\Ac}$ as if it were $\phi_1$. This does not affect
$\Tm_{11}$, which is quadratic in $\phi_1$, but it flips the sign and rescales
$\Tm_{12}$ once $h\neq0$.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The response to a thermal drive is carried not by
$\delta E_{\rm int}$ but by $\bs^\top\mathsf A^{-1}\bs$ --- the \emph{inverse}-weighted
occupation, which loads most heavily on the soft modes rather than least. That inversion is
the whole effect: the constraint converts an observable with vanishing soft-mode weight into
a response function dominated by them.

\subsection{The result}
From \eqref{eq:twoforms} with $\nabla\Gc=2\mathsf A^{-1}\bs$,
\begin{equation}
  |\nabla_M\Gc|^2=4\Big[\bs^\top\mathsf A^{-2}\bs-\tfrac1N\big(\bs^\top\mathsf A^{-1}\bs\big)^2\Big].
\end{equation}
Here $\avg{\bs^\top\mathsf A^{-2}\bs}=\tfrac n2NI_3$, while
\begin{equation}
  \frac1N\avg{\big(\bs^\top\mathsf A^{-1}\bs\big)^2}
   =\frac1N\Big[\big(\tfrac n2NI_2\big)^2+\mathrm{Var}(\Gc)\Big]
   =NI_2^2+O(I_4)\qquad(n=2),
\end{equation}
the remainder being $\tfrac n2 I_4$, \emph{not} $O(1)$ uniformly in $r$: $I_4\sim r^{-5/2}$.
Relative to the retained $I_3-I_2^2\sim r^{-3/2}$ it is $O(1/Nr)$, weaker than
\eqref{eq:control}, so \eqref{eq:control} remains the binding condition. Hence, per site,
\begin{equation}
  \frac1N\avg{|\nabla_M\Gc|^2}=4\big(I_3-I_2^2\big)+O\!\Big(\frac{I_4}{N}\Big),
  \qquad
  \Tm_{11}=\Gamma\,\frac{\tfrac1N\avg{|\nabla_M\Gc|^2}}{(4\Gamma\beta I_2)^2}
  =\frac{1}{4\Gamma\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{I_3}{I_2^{2}}-1\right),
\end{equation}
using $I_1=1$, which is \eqref{eq:T11}.

\paragraph{Positivity is automatic.} By Cauchy--Schwarz,
$I_2=\int a^{-1/2}a^{-3/2}\le\sqrt{I_1I_3}=\sqrt{I_3}$, with equality only if $a_{\bk}$ is
constant. Writing $d\rho=a_{\bk}^{-1}\dk$ (a probability measure since $I_1=1$),
\begin{equation}
  \frac{I_3}{I_2^2}-1=\frac{\avg{a^{-2}}_\rho-\avg{a^{-1}}_\rho^2}{\avg{a^{-1}}_\rho^{2}}
   =\frac{\mathrm{Var}_\rho(a^{-1})}{\avg{a^{-1}}_\rho^{2}} :
\end{equation}
a relative variance. The response measures the \emph{spread} of inverse mode stiffnesses,
not their mean --- which is why it is invisible to any calculation tracking only
$\avg{a^{-1}}$.

\subsection{The magnetisation sector}
For a linear observable $F=\vec u\cdot\bs$ with $\vec u$ an eigenvector of $\mathsf A$, the
degree-1 cancellation of \S\ref{sec:quad} leaves $-\Lc F=2\Gamma a_{\bk}F$. Taking $\vec u$
uniform ($\bk=0$, eigenvalue $a_0$),
\begin{equation}
  \Tm_{22}=\frac{\mathrm{Var}(X_2)/N}{2\Gamma a_0}=\frac{1}{4\Gamma a_0^{2}} ,
\end{equation}
within the same leading-$N$ reduction. Since $a_0=r+2\beta P$ is non-critical, the field
direction is regular and the divergence lives entirely in $\Tm_{11}$, matching $g_{tt}$ in
[BR, Eq.~(11)].

\FLAG{Which symmetry gives $\Tm_{12}=0$.} At $h=0$ it is $\sigma^z_j\to-\sigma^z_j$ (or
global $\bs\to-\bs$), under which $E_{\rm int}$ is even and $M_{\rm tot}$ odd. It is
\emph{not} the reflection $\sigma^x\to-\sigma^x$ used elsewhere in these notes, under which
both observables are even. (An earlier draft cited the wrong one.)

\subsection{Critical behaviour}
\label{sec:crit4}
With $a_{\bQ-\bq}=r+cq^2$ and $\int\!\dk=\tfrac{1}{2\pi^2}\int q^2dq$, the substitution
$q=\sqrt{r/c}\,u$ gives
\begin{equation}
  I_2\simeq\frac{1}{8\pi c^{3/2}}\,r^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  I_3\simeq\frac{1}{32\pi c^{3/2}}\,r^{-3/2},
  \qquad
  I_4\simeq\frac{1}{64\pi c^{3/2}}\,r^{-5/2},
\end{equation}
using $\int_0^\infty u^2(1+u^2)^{-2}du=\pi/4$, $\int u^2(1+u^2)^{-3}du=\pi/16$,
$\int u^2(1+u^2)^{-4}du=\pi/32$. Hence
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \frac{I_3}{I_2^{2}}\simeq 2\pi c^{3/2}\,r^{-1/2}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \Tm_{11}\simeq\frac{\pi c^{3/2}}{2\Gamma\beta^{2}}\,r^{-1/2}\ }
\end{equation}
and with $r\sim\epsilon^2$, $\Tm_{11}\sim|\epsilon|^{-1}$ and
$\sqrt{\Tm_{11}}\sim|\epsilon|^{-1/2}$, integrable.

\paragraph{Numerical check.} Evaluating $I_p$ on a $220^3$ zone grid with $\Jc=1$, $\beta$
re-solved at each $r$ so that $I_1=1$. Each row is compared with its \emph{own}
$2\pi c(r)^{3/2}$, since $c=\beta\Jc/2$ drifts with $r$:

\hline
$3\times10^{-2}$ & $0.4447$ & $2.547$  & $0.441$ & $0.659$ & $0.570$\\
$1\times10^{-2}$ & $0.4718$ & $5.676$  & $0.568$ & $0.720$ & $0.725$\\

The limiting prediction is $2\pi c_c^{3/2}=0.7983$ at

\paragraph{Consistency with [BR].} $b(1-z_{\rm dyn}/D)=1$ gives $g_{tt}\sim|\epsilon|^{-1}$,
which is what \eqref{eq:T11} produces. The scaling criterion of [BR,\S c] is therefore
\emph{supported} by this calculation, rather than contradicted as the earlier draft
suggested. Following the referee, we say ``obtained analytically as the leading
thermodynamic-limit result for the projected dynamics on the disordered side'' rather than
``confirmed by an exactly solved model'': \eqref{eq:control} shows the derivation is not
uniform at the critical point.

\paragraph{Where the $\alpha=-1$ cusp went.} The static Hessian has only a vanishing cusp
$\sim|\epsilon|$ (\S3.8), while $\Tm_{11}$ diverges as $|\epsilon|^{-1}$; the ratio is
$|\epsilon|^{-2}=\tau$. So the factorisation
$\Tm\sim(\text{singular susceptibility})\times\tau$ does hold --- but only because the
resolvent \eqref{eq:phiA} selects the singular piece. Nothing in the equal-time covariance
announces which piece will be multiplied by the slow time.

\subsection{Which functional is being minimised}
\label{sec:objective}
$\Tm_{\mu\nu}$ of \eqref{eq:Tdef} is the time-integrated covariance in the natural
(Massieu) coordinates $\lambda=(\beta,h)$. The manuscript's metric differs by a scalar
factor, and the notes should not conflate them. The factor is \emph{not} settled, and the
two candidates differ by $\beta^2$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Reading (i), $g=\Tm/\beta$.} Sivak--Crooks with an energy-like field
        $\Lambda$ ($\Uc=\Uc_0-\beta\Lambda X$) gives
        $\zeta_{\Lambda\Lambda}=\beta\!\int\!\avg{\delta X\delta X}dt$ with
        $\avg{W_{\rm ex}}=\int\dot\Lambda\zeta\dot\Lambda\,dt$. Changing to $h=\beta\Lambda$
        at fixed $\beta$ transforms as a tensor,
        $\zeta_{hh}=\zeta_{\Lambda\Lambda}(\partial\Lambda/\partial h)^2=\Tm_{hh}/\beta$.
        Cross-check: availability $\simeq T\,\Delta S_{\rm ex}$ and
        $\Delta S_{\rm ex}=\int\Tm_{\mu\nu}\dot\lambda^\mu\dot\lambda^\nu dt$ in natural
        coordinates, giving $\Tm/\beta$ again.
  \item \textbf{Reading (ii), $g=\beta\Tm$.} Read [BR, Eq.~(1)] literally as
        $g=\beta\!\int\!\avg{\delta X\delta X}dt$ with $\lambda$ already the natural
        coordinates, so that $\partial^2\psi/\partial\lambda\partial\lambda=\mathrm{Cov}$ and
        the $\beta$ is an extra scalar.
\end{itemize}
Reading (i) is the one the tensor transformation supports; reading (ii) is the literal text
of [BR, Eq.~(1)]. Either way the \emph{exponent} is unaffected, since $\beta_c$ is finite
and the factor is smooth. But lengths, curvature, and geodesics all depend on it, so
[BR,\S b] must state which functional is being minimised --- excess work, dissipated
availability, or excess entropy production --- before \S5 computes any minimal paths.

\subsection{Static consistency check}
\label{sec:staticcheck}
The $t=0$ limit of the same fluctuation-space reduction must reproduce the exact
thermodynamic-limit statics of \S3. On the \emph{disordered} branch at $h=0$ --- which is
the relevant target here, not the ordered-phase covariances --- the Schur complement of
\S3.7 gives, with $R=0$, $B=A/\beta$, $D_z=I_2$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{Var}(X_1)}{N}=\frac{1}{\beta^2}\Big[C-\frac{A^2}{I_2}\Big]
   =\frac{1}{\beta^2}\Big[I_0-\frac{I_1^2}{I_2}\Big]
   =\boxed{\ \frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{I_2}\Big)\ }
\end{equation}
where the second equality is an identity: with $A=I_1-rI_2$ and $C=I_0-2rI_1+r^2I_2$, all
explicit $r$-dependence cancels. Likewise $\mathrm{Var}(X_2)/N=1/2a_0$ and
$\mathrm{Cov}(X_1,X_2)/N=0$. As $r\to0$, $I_2\to\infty$ and
$\mathrm{Var}(X_1)/N\to1/\beta^2$, matching the ordered-side value \eqref{eq:ordhess} of
\S3.2 --- the continuity established there. The numerical column $1-1/I_2$ in
\S\ref{sec:crit4} shows this approach.

\textbf{Result of this section.} On the $h=0$ ordered branch, in the thermodynamic limit

\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \Tm_{11}^{\rm ord}=\frac{t}{4\Gamma\beta^{2}x^{2}}+o\!\big(x^{-2}\big)\ }

The divergence arises from the \emph{rank-one inversion denominator} $1-\Wc=x^2$

  \Tm_{11}^{\rm ord}\to\frac{1}{4\Gamma\beta_c^{2}|\epsilon|},

for the projected dynamics considered here. \FLAG{Not claimed to be universal along the

\subsection{Why \S4 cannot simply be continued}
The disordered-side result $\Tm_{11}=\frac{1}{4\Gamma\beta^2}(I_3/I_2^2-1)$ is built from
$I_p=\int\!\dk\,a_{\bk}^{-p}$. In the ordered phase $r=0$ identically, so
$a_{\bQ-\bq}=cq^2$ and
\begin{equation}
  I_1\ \text{converges},
  \qquad
  I_2\sim\int\!\frac{d^3q}{q^4},\quad I_3\sim\int\!\frac{d^3q}{q^6}\ \text{diverge in the infrared}
\end{equation}
($I_2\simeq L/4\pi^3c^2$ in a finite box). The formula does not merely become large; its
ingredients cease to exist. The reason is the one flagged in \S2.2 and \S3.4: at $r=0$ the
$\bQ$-mode is a macroscopically occupied condensate, not a small Gaussian fluctuation, and
must be separated first.

Most of \S4 survives: the generator, the sphere geometry, the curvature/mean-force
cancellation, and --- at $h=0$ --- the vanishing of the drift along the $Q_-$ normal, since
the identity $\bs^\top\mathsf A\Em\bs=zQ_-=0$ of \S4.3 used only $\{\mathsf K,\Em\}=0$ and
$Q_-=0$. \FLAG{But see \S\ref{sec:Qminus}:} that identity handles the \emph{drift}; the
second constraint also modifies the Laplace--Beltrami operator, so the statements below hold
to leading thermodynamic order in the sublattice-symmetric sector, not exactly.

\subsection{The condensate is the constraint reservoir}
Since $\bQ\equiv-\bQ$, $\bm S_{\bQ}$ is real; write
\begin{equation}
  Q'\equiv\sum_{\bk\neq\bQ}|\bm S_{\bk}|^2 ,
  \qquad\text{so that}\qquad
  Q_+=Q'+|\bm S_{\bQ}|^2=N .
  \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{(i) The condensate carries no weight in $\Ac$.} With
$\Ac=\bs^\top\mathsf A\bs=\sum_{\bk}a_{\bk}|\bm S_{\bk}|^2$ and $a_{\bQ}=0$,
\begin{equation}
  \Ac=\sum_{\bk\neq\bQ}a_{\bk}|\bm S_{\bk}|^2 .
\end{equation}
\FLAG{Say this carefully.} It is $\Ac$, not the physical energy, that has no $\bQ$-weight.
The interaction energy does contain $\Jc(\bQ)|\bm S_{\bQ}|^2=-P|\bm S_{\bQ}|^2$; the
condensate is not free. What is true is that on the constraint surface
\begin{equation}
  \Ac=\beta E_{\rm int}+zQ_+
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \delta\Ac=\beta\,\delta E_{\rm int}\quad\text{since }\delta Q_+=0 ,
\end{equation}
so the energy \emph{fluctuation} can be represented with no direct weight on the condensate.
Changing the condensate occupation still costs energy; that cost is exactly offset by the
compensating rearrangement of the non-condensate modes that the constraint enforces.

\paragraph{(ii) The condensate absorbs the constraint.} From \eqref{eq:split},
\begin{equation}
  \delta Q'=-\,\delta\big(|\bm S_{\bQ}|^2\big)\neq0 .
  \label{eq:reservoir}
\end{equation}
In the \emph{disordered} phase $\delta Q_+=0$ made $\vec b=\vec1$ redundant (\S4.5). Here
the sum over $\bk\neq\bQ$ is not zero --- it is minus the condensate fluctuation --- so the
redundancy is gone.

\subsection{Why the non-condensate modes are Gaussian}
\label{sec:gaussian}
The claim that the condensate acts as a reservoir leaving the rest effectively
unconstrained deserves a derivation, and it turns out to be special to the planar case.
Integrating out the $n$-component $\bQ$-mode against the constraint,
\begin{equation}
  \int d^{n}S_{\bQ}\;\delta\big(|\bm S_{\bQ}|^2+Q'-N\big)
   =\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}\,\big(N-Q'\big)^{n/2-1}\,\Theta\big(N-Q'\big) .
\end{equation}
For $n=2$ the exponent is $n/2-1=0$, so the factor is simply $\pi\,\Theta(N-Q')$ and the
marginal measure of the non-condensate modes is
\begin{equation}
  d\mu'\ \propto\ \exp\Big[-\sum_{\bk\neq\bQ}a_{\bk}|\bm S_{\bk}|^2\Big]\,\Theta(N-Q')\,dS' :
\end{equation}
\emph{exactly Gaussian}, apart from a hard cutoff $Q'\le N$ which sits
$\sim Nx^2/\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(Q')}$ standard deviations away and is therefore irrelevant
outside the finite-size critical window (\S\ref{sec:validity}). For general $n$ the residual
factor $(N-Q')^{n/2-1}$ would survive and this argument would need modifying.

\subsection{The operator, and the rank-one denominator}
\label{sec:lift}
Restrict to $F=\sum_{\bk\neq\bQ}b_{\bk}y_{\bk}$. The derivation of \S4.4 goes through ---
note $\avg{\Ac}=\tfrac n2(N-1)\simeq\tfrac{nN}{2}$ still holds, the condensate contributing
zero to $\Ac$ --- giving
\begin{equation}
  \Mc\,\vec b=4\Gamma\Big[\vec a\odot\vec b-\frac{n}{2N}\big(\vec b\cdot\vec a^{-1}\big)\vec a\Big],
  \qquad \bk\neq\bQ ,
  \label{eq:Mord}
\end{equation}
with $\avg{F}=\tfrac n2\sum_{\bk\neq\bQ}b_{\bk}/a_{\bk}$ evaluated with the Gaussian
occupations of \S\ref{sec:gaussian}.

\paragraph{Matrix form.} For $n=2$, write $D_{\vec a}=\mathrm{diag}(a_{\bk})$ and
$u_{\bk}=1/(Na_{\bk})$. Then \eqref{eq:Mord} is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \frac{\Mc}{4\Gamma}=D_{\vec a}\big(\mathsf I-\vec1\,\vec u^{\top}\big),
  \qquad \vec u^{\top}\vec1=\frac1N\sum_{\bk\neq\bQ}\frac{1}{a_{\bk}}=\Wc=t\ }
  \label{eq:rankone}
\end{equation}
and by the matrix determinant lemma
\begin{equation}
  \det\big(\mathsf I-\vec1\,\vec u^{\top}\big)=1-\vec u^\top\vec1=1-t=x^{2} .
  \label{eq:detlemma}
\end{equation}
\textbf{This is the origin of the $1/x^2$.} The order parameter is the determinant of the
rank-one factor: as $x\to0$ the operator $\Mc$ becomes singular and its inverse blows up.

\FLAG{Not a soft eigenmode.} An earlier draft said $\vec1$ is a mode whose ``stiffness'' is
$x^2$, on the strength of $\Mc\vec1=4\Gamma x^2\vec a$. That is wrong: the right-hand side
is proportional to $\vec a$, not to $\vec1$, so $\vec1$ is \emph{not} an eigenvector and
$4\Gamma x^2$ is \emph{not} a relaxation rate. The singularity is in the determinant
\eqref{eq:detlemma}, not in an isolated eigenvalue. See \S\ref{sec:rates} for what this
means for $z_{\rm dyn}$.

\subsection{Solving the resolvent}
Solve $\Mc\vec\phi=\vec a$ --- with no $\kappa\vec1$ freedom, since there is no redundancy.
With $c_\phi\equiv\tfrac{n}{2N}\sum_{\bk\neq\bQ}\phi_{\bk}/a_{\bk}$,
\begin{equation}
  4\Gamma\big[a_{\bk}\phi_{\bk}-c_\phi a_{\bk}\big]=a_{\bk}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \phi_{\bk}=c_\phi+\frac{1}{4\Gamma}\quad\text{--- independent of }\bk ,
\end{equation}
and self-consistency, using $\tfrac n2\Wc=t$,
\begin{equation}
  c_\phi=\Big(c_\phi+\frac{1}{4\Gamma}\Big)t
  \ \Longrightarrow\
  c_\phi=\frac{t}{4\Gamma(1-t)}=\frac{t}{4\Gamma x^{2}},
  \qquad
  \phi_{\bk}=\frac{1}{4\Gamma}\cdot\frac{t+x^{2}}{x^{2}}=\frac{1}{4\Gamma x^{2}} ,
\end{equation}
using $t+x^2=1$. The solution is a \emph{constant} vector, so by \eqref{eq:reservoir}
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\
  \phi_{\Ac}\equiv(-\Lc)^{-1}\delta\Ac
   =\frac{\delta Q'}{4\Gamma x^{2}}
   =-\frac{1}{4\Gamma x^{2}}\,\delta\big(|\bm S_{\bQ}|^{2}\big)\ }
  \label{eq:phiord}
\end{equation}
and, since $\delta X_1=-\beta^{-1}\delta\Ac$ (\S4.6),
\begin{equation}
  \phi_1=-\frac{1}{\beta}\phi_{\Ac}=\frac{1}{4\Gamma\beta x^{2}}\,\delta\big(|\bm S_{\bQ}|^{2}\big).
  \label{eq:phi1ord}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The variable carrying the zero-frequency response to a thermal
drive is the condensate occupation. This complements \S4: on the disordered side the
resolvent produced $\bs^\top\mathsf A^{-1}\bs$, dominated by the modes near $\bQ$; on the
ordered side those modes have condensed, and the response collapses onto the single
macroscopic variable they became. In both phases the answer is carried by the soft sector,
which $\delta\Ac$ itself does not weight.

\FLAG{The $O(2)$ sector at $h=0$.} At finite $N$ the projected diffusion is elliptic on a
compact connected manifold, so its only exact zero eigenfunctions are constants: the global
orientation of $\bm S_{\bQ}$ in the plane is a \emph{slow rotational diffusion} whose
eigenvalue vanishes with system size, not an exact zero mode. (An earlier draft claimed an
exact zero eigenvalue; that holds only after the thermodynamic limit or pure-state
selection.) The useful statement is unaffected: both $\delta\Ac$ and
$\delta|\bm S_{\bQ}|^2$ are $O(2)$-invariant, so they lie in the angular-momentum-zero
sector and have no overlap with the rotational modes. In the broken-symmetry limit this
becomes orthogonality to the Goldstone mode.

\subsection{The result, two ways}
\paragraph{Route 1: mode sum.} With the Gaussian measure of \S\ref{sec:gaussian},
$\avg{y_{\bk}y_{\bk'}}=\delta_{\bk\bk'}n/2a_{\bk}^2$, so
\begin{equation}
  \avg{\delta\Ac\,\phi_{\Ac}}=\frac{1}{4\Gamma x^{2}}\sum_{\bk\neq\bQ}a_{\bk}\frac{n}{2a_{\bk}^{2}}
   =\frac{1}{4\Gamma x^{2}}\cdot\frac n2N\Wc=\frac{Nt}{4\Gamma x^{2}}\quad(n=2),
\end{equation}
and $\Tm_{11}=\beta^{-2}\avg{\delta\Ac\,\phi_{\Ac}}/N$ gives \eqref{eq:T11ord}.

\paragraph{Route 2: Dirichlet form.} Write $Q'=\bs^\top\mathsf B\bs$ with
$\mathsf B=\mathsf I-\Pi_{\bQ}$, $\mathsf B^2=\mathsf B$. With $\Pp=\mathsf I-\bs\bs^\top/N$,
the \emph{pointwise} identity is
\begin{equation}
  |\nabla_M Q'|^{2}=4\Big[Q'-\frac{Q'^{2}}{N}\Big]=4N\,q\,(1-q),
  \qquad q\equiv\frac{Q'}{N} ,
\end{equation}
where $q$ \emph{fluctuates}; only its mean is $t=1-x^2$. (An earlier draft substituted
$q=1-x^2$ pointwise.) Averaging,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \avg{|\nabla_MQ'|^{2}}=4N\big[\avg q-\avg{q^2}\big]
   =4Ntx^{2}-\frac{4}{N}\mathrm{Var}(Q')\ }
\end{equation}
Since $\mathrm{Var}(Q')\sim NL$ (\S\ref{sec:validity}), the relative correction is
$\mathrm{Var}(Q')/N^2tx^2\sim1/x^2L^2$, which vanishes faster than the closure error
$1/x^2L$. Hence $\avg{|\nabla_MQ'|^2}=4Ntx^2+o(Nx^2)$ in the iterated limit, and
\begin{equation}
  \Tm_{11}=\frac{\Gamma}{N}\,\frac{\avg{|\nabla_MQ'|^{2}}}{(4\Gamma\beta x^{2})^{2}}
   =\frac{t}{4\Gamma\beta^{2}x^{2}}+o\big(x^{-2}\big) \qquad\checkmark
\end{equation}
The two routes agree. Note $q(1-q)$ is a geometric factor from projecting the gradient onto
the sphere (the Bernoulli variance function evaluated at $q$), not literally the variance of
an occupation fraction.

\paragraph{Magnetisation sector.} $a_0=2\beta P$ is non-critical, so
$\Tm_{22}=1/4\Gamma a_0^2=1/16\Gamma\beta^2P^2$ and $\Tm_{12}=0$ at $h=0$. The divergence is
confined to the thermal direction.

\subsection{The $Q_-$ constraint: what is and is not shown}
\label{sec:Qminus}
Using the full $\Pt$ adds $-\tfrac4N(\bs^\top\Em\mathsf B\bs)^2$ to the Dirichlet form.
Here $\bs^\top\Em\mathsf B\bs=Q_--\bm S_{\bQ}\cdot\bm S_0=-\bm S_{\bQ}\cdot\bm S_0\simeq-\sqrt Nx\,S^x_0$
at $h=0$, so the extra term is $-4x^2\avg{(S^x_0)^2}=-x^2/\beta P$: $O(1)$ against
$4Ntx^2=O(N)$, hence negligible.

\FLAG{This is not a proof.} Checking the Dirichlet norm of a trial function does not show
that \eqref{eq:phiord} solves the Poisson equation for the \emph{full} two-constraint
generator: the second constraint modifies the Laplace--Beltrami operator, not only the
projected norm. The clean remaining check is to evaluate the residual
\begin{equation}
  R\equiv-\Lc_{\rm full}\Big(\frac{\delta Q'}{4\Gamma x^{2}}\Big)-\delta\Ac
  \qquad\text{and show}\qquad
  \frac{\|R\|}{\|\delta\Ac\|}\to0\ \text{ for } x^2L\gg1 .
\end{equation}
The drift correction vanishes exactly (\S4.3) and the geometric correction should be
subleading, so this is expected to work --- but until it is done, the results of this
section hold ``to leading thermodynamic order in the sublattice-symmetric sector''.

\subsection{Critical behaviour and the tip amplitude ratio}
\label{sec:ratio}
Fix $\epsilon\equiv t-1$, positive on the disordered side.

\paragraph{Ordered side.} $x^2=1-t=|\epsilon|$ directly from \eqref{eq:x2}, so
$\Tm_{11}^{\rm ord}\to1/4\Gamma\beta_c^2|\epsilon|$. (Note $x\sim|\epsilon|^{1/2}$, i.e.\
$\beta_{\rm op}=\tfrac12$ \checkmark.)

\paragraph{Disordered side.} Convert $r$ to $\epsilon$ using the Watson cusp of \S2.5:
$\Wc(r)=\Wc(0)-C_1\sqrt r$ with $\Wc(0)=t$, $C_1=1/4\pi c^{3/2}$, and the constraint
$\Wc(r)=1$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \sqrt r=\frac{\epsilon}{C_1}=4\pi c^{3/2}\epsilon\ }
  \label{eq:reps}
\end{equation}
Substituting into $I_3/I_2^2\simeq2\pi c^{3/2}r^{-1/2}$ from \S4.8 gives
$I_3/I_2^2\simeq1/2\epsilon$ --- every factor of $c$ cancels --- and
$\Tm_{11}^{\rm dis}\simeq1/8\Gamma\beta_c^2\epsilon$.

\paragraph{The ratio.} Hence $A_-/A_+=2$ at the tip, independent of $\Gamma$, $\beta_c$,
$c$, $\Jc$ and the lattice, and unchanged by the smooth scalar relating $\Tm$ to $g$. It is
the sharpest available check on the construction: the two sides come from structurally
different calculations --- a Brillouin-zone integral versus an occupation variance --- and
the lattice-dependent constants must cancel between them.

\FLAG{Do not call this universal along the critical line.} At $h\neq0$ the counting in
\eqref{eq:rankone} becomes $\vec u^\top\vec1=\Wc=1-M^2-x^2$, so
\begin{equation}
  \Mc\vec1=4\Gamma\,\vec a\,\big(M^{2}+x^{2}\big),
\end{equation}
and on the critical line $x=0$ but $M_c^2>0$. \textbf{The constant sector therefore does not
become singular at a generic boundary point.} Whatever divergence occurs there must come
from the coupling to the uniform linear mode $v=\delta S^z_0$ and to the $Q_-$ sector --- it
is a different mechanism, not the substitution $x^2\to$ (some other vanishing scalar). The
$h=0$ tip is also the one point where $O(2)$ is restored, so it is not representative.
Establishing whether the ratio $2$ survives at $H_c\neq0$ is a genuine open calculation.

\paragraph{Numerical support (partial).} On an $N_k^3=300^3$ zone grid ($\Jc=1$,
$\beta_0^{\rm grid}=0.5045$ against the exact $0.505462$), the two disordered-side
ingredients:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
$r$ & $4\pi c^{3/2}\epsilon\big/\sqrt r$ \ \eqref{eq:reps} & $8\pi c^{3/2}I_2\sqrt r$ \\
\hline
$3\times10^{-2}$ & $1.023$ & $1.061$ \\
$1\times10^{-2}$ & $0.998$ & $1.047$ \\
$3\times10^{-3}$ & $0.963$ & $1.029$ \\
$1\times10^{-3}$ & $0.917$ & $1.018$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both should tend to $1$. Resolving the infrared requires
$q_{\min}=2\pi/N_k\ll\sqrt{r/c}$, i.e.\ $N_k\gg2\pi\sqrt{c/r}\approx99$ at $r=10^{-3}$
($c\simeq0.25$); with $N_k=300$ the margin is only a factor $\simeq3$, which is why the last
row drifts. The ordered side is exact algebra and needs no numerics. A direct simulation of
the projected dynamics would be the real test.

\subsection{Relaxation rates and $z_{\rm dyn}$}
\label{sec:rates}
Because $\vec1$ is not an eigenvector of $\Mc$ (\S\ref{sec:lift}), nothing here identifies an
isolated mode of rate $4\Gamma x^2$. The long-wavelength modes still relax at
\begin{equation}
  4\Gamma a_{\bQ-\bq}=4\Gamma c\,q^{2},
\end{equation}
consistent with $z_{\rm dyn}=2$, and the system remains gapless throughout the ordered
phase. The correct statement is that the \emph{integrated autocorrelation time} of the
thermal observable scales as $x^{-2}$, through the rank-one denominator, while the spectral
gap is controlled by the $q^2$ continuum. The dynamical exponent must still be extracted
separately, and the dagger on the Table~I row of \S3.11 stands.

\subsection{Finite length across the transition}
\label{sec:length}
$\Tm_{11}$ is a component in the coordinate $\beta$, and the manuscript's metric differs
from $\Tm$ by a smooth scalar (\S4.11). Both must be handled before integrating in
$\epsilon$:
\begin{equation}
  g_{\epsilon\epsilon}=g_{\beta\beta}\Big(\frac{d\beta}{d\epsilon}\Big)^{2},
  \qquad
  \beta=\frac{\beta_0}{1+\epsilon},
  \qquad
  \frac{d\beta}{d\epsilon}=-\frac{\beta_0}{(1+\epsilon)^{2}} .
\end{equation}
The Jacobian and the scalar are both smooth and nonzero at $\epsilon=0$, so
$g_{\epsilon\epsilon}\sim A_\pm|\epsilon|^{-1}$ with the same exponent, and
\begin{equation}
  \int_{-\epsilon_0}^{+\epsilon_0}\sqrt{g_{\epsilon\epsilon}}\;d\epsilon
  \ \sim\ \big(\sqrt{A_-}+\sqrt{A_+}\big)\int_0^{\epsilon_0}\frac{d\epsilon}{\sqrt\epsilon}
  \ <\ \infty .
\end{equation}
The metric diverges; the length does not. This is the phenomenon [BR] describes, now in an
exactly solved $D=3$ model, with the divergence obtained on \emph{both} sides rather than
inferred on one.

\FLAG{Scope.} This is the $h=0$ crossing, entering the ordered tongue through its tip. A
generic crossing at $H_c\neq0$ is not covered, for the reason in \S\ref{sec:ratio}.

\subsection{Validity}
\label{sec:validity}
With $\phi_{\Ac}\propto\delta Q'$ and
$\mathrm{Var}(Q')=\tfrac n2N\!\int'\!a_{\bk}^{-2}\simeq\tfrac n2N\cdot L/4\pi^3c^2$,
\begin{equation}
  \delta\phi_{\Ac}\sim\frac{\sqrt{NL}}{x^{2}},
  \qquad
  \underbrace{\delta\Ac}_{\text{retained}}\sim\sqrt N,
  \qquad
  \underbrace{\tfrac{4\Gamma}{N}\delta\Ac\,\delta\phi_{\Ac}}_{\text{dropped}}\sim\frac{\sqrt L}{x^{2}},
\end{equation}
so the linear closure is controlled for
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ x^{2}L\gg1\ }
\end{equation}
\paragraph{A consistency check.} The same condition follows from requiring the condensate
reservoir to sit far from the cutoff $Q'\le N$ of \S\ref{sec:gaussian}:
\begin{equation}
  Nx^{2}\gg\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(Q')}\sim\sqrt{NL}
  \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
  Nx^{4}\gg L
  \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
  x^{2}L\gg1 ,
\end{equation}
using $N=L^3$. Two independent routes to the same inequality.

\paragraph{Which length is this?} \FLAG{Not the correlation length.} Since $r=0$ throughout
the ordered phase the propagator is massless and $\xi=\infty$ everywhere below the
transition; an earlier draft wrote $x^2\sim\xi^{-1}$, which contradicts that. The right
object is the thermal crossover length
\begin{equation}
  \ell_\epsilon\equiv|\epsilon|^{-\nu}=|\epsilon|^{-1},
  \qquad\text{so that}\qquad
  x^2L\gg1\iff L\gg\ell_\epsilon .
\end{equation}
With this reading the condition matches $Nr^{3/2}\gg1$ on the disordered side (\S4.4) and
the static saddle-point condition (\S2.3): the whole construction --- statics, disordered
dynamics, ordered dynamics --- has one validity domain, $L\gg\ell_\epsilon$, with the
thermodynamic limit taken before the critical limit. Worth stating once, plainly, in the
manuscript.

\paragraph{Superextensive constraint variance.} $\mathrm{Var}(Q')\sim NL$ means, via
\eqref{eq:reservoir}, that the condensate amplitude fluctuates as
$\avg{u^2}\sim N^{1/3}/x^2$ with $u=S^x_{\bQ}-\sqrt Nx$ --- the usual soft-condensate
behaviour, subleading against $\sqrt Nx$ precisely when $x^2L\gg1$.

\subsection{What remains}
\label{sec:remaining5}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{$h\neq0$ --- a different calculation, not a substitution.} By
        \S\ref{sec:ratio}, the constant sector stays regular on the critical line when
        $M_c\neq0$. The generic-point problem requires the full two-constraint Poisson
        equation with the uniform linear mode $v$ in the space alongside the bilinears, and
        whatever divergence occurs must emerge from that coupling. This is the main open
        item, and it is harder than \S5.
  \item \textbf{The residual $R$} of \S\ref{sec:Qminus}, to upgrade the trial function to a
        genuine solution of the full generator.
  \item \textbf{Whether $A_-/A_+=2$ survives at $H_c\neq0$.} If it does, it is a strong
        result in its own right; if not, it is a property of the tip and must be quoted as
        such.
  \item \textbf{\S6: minimal paths}, once the objective functional of \S4.11 is settled ---
        the conversion factor changes geodesics, not just lengths.
\end{enumerate}
